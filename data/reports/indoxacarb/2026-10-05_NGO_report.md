## 1. Summary

Between 2024-10-05 and 2026-10-05, no NGO ran a campaign, legal action, letter or test aimed at indoxacarb. Nothing NGOs published touches its biocidal (PT18) use, such as ant and cockroach gel baits. The only verified in-window mention is a passing one: Générations Futures' December 2024 food-residue report names indoxacarb as a pesticide the EU has already banned. NGO attention was strongest in 2022–2024, when the EU had stopped approving it as a farm pesticide (not renewed in 2021). PAN Europe and Nature & Progrès Belgium contested a Belgian emergency use in 2023. GLOBAL 2000, BUND and PAN Europe found indoxacarb residues on plants sold as "bee-friendly". Foodwatch NL reported residues on children's fruit and raisins. Overall direction: dormant. Indoxacarb now appears in NGO material mainly as an example of an EU-banned pesticide, not as an active target.

## 2. Top signals (ranked by likely impact on the BPR renewal)

No in-window item rises above low impact. The one verified in-window item is listed below. One older item is added as clearly labelled background, because it shows how NGOs frame indoxacarb.

**1. 2024-12-17: Générations Futures lists indoxacarb as a pesticide already banned in the EU, in a report on pesticide residues in French non-organic food**
- Summary: The French NGO's analysis of official residue-monitoring data names indoxacarb as an example of a substance banned in December 2021, with stocks to be used up by September 2022. It appears as an example of how the regulatory status changed after the data year, not as a campaign target.
- Why it matters for the BPR renewal: Indirect and weak. It reinforces the framing that indoxacarb is "banned in the EU". That framing could be used against keeping its biocidal approval, but the item raises no hazard argument of its own. The underlying non-renewal under the farm-pesticide rules rested mainly on exposure-specific risks: wild mammals, bees, and consumers and workers for lettuce use. Those carry over only weakly to indoor gel baits.
- NGO: Générations Futures, https://www.generations-futures.fr/publications/residus-pesticides-2/

**Background (outside window, for context only): 2023-01-31 / 2023-02-28: PAN Europe and Nature & Progrès Belgium demanded that Belgium cancel an emergency authorisation for an indoxacarb insecticide, arguing that EU-banned pesticides cannot be re-authorised**
- Summary: Belgium granted a temporary authorisation for indoxacarb (ornamental plants in greenhouses) on 2023-01-19. The NGOs wrote to the agriculture minister. On 2023-02-27 they sent a formal notice threatening court action, citing the CJEU ruling of 2023-01-19 on emergency derogations for banned substances.
- Why it matters: Indirect. It shows that NGOs treat indoxacarb as a substance banned "for health or environmental reasons". That narrative could be revived if the biocide renewal attracts attention. It concerns farm-pesticide derogations only, not biocides. No follow-up on indoxacarb was found in the window.
- NGOs: PAN Europe, https://www.pan-europe.info/blog/groundbreaking-eu-court-ruling-should-stop-all-highly-toxic-pesticide-derogations-%E2%80%93-now ; Nature & Progrès Belgium, https://www.natpro.be/pesticides-nature-progres-et-pan-europe-mettent-en-demeure-la-belgique/

## 5. What NGOs say is coming next

Nothing announced on indoxacarb specifically. Two class-level campaigns could pull it in:
- PAN Europe, foodwatch and the Veblen Institute are campaigning for an automatic ban on residues of non-approved pesticides in imported food, and for MEPs and the Council to reject the Food Safety Omnibus (2026-04-21, 2026-05-19). Indoxacarb is non-approved for farm use, so its import tolerances fall within that push.

No NGO has signalled engagement with the BPR PT18 renewal, which expires 2026-12-31 (already postponed) with France evaluating.
