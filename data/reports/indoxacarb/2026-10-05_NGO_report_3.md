## 1. Summary

1 of 1 top signals targets the plant protection (PPP) side, and none target the biocide approval directly. NGO activity on indoxacarb in the window is very thin. The EU stopped approving it as a plant protection product in 2021, and NGOs have since treated it as one more "banned bee-killer" that turns up in residue tests and lists. They have not run a campaign on it. The only verified in-window item is an October 2025 article by the Dutch Bijenstichting (Bee Foundation). It covers a Europe-wide study of bumblebee colonies published in Nature in April 2024 and names indoxacarb among the nine most dangerous residues found. A Greenpeace Austria spice residue test (May 2025) reportedly detected indoxacarb, but we could not confirm this on Greenpeace's own pages. We found no NGO comment on the BPR PT18 renewal (France is the evaluating authority), the 2025 RAC/CLH opinion, the veterinary flea product, or indoxacarb's fluorinated (trifluoromethoxy) structure in the PFAS-pesticide debate. Overall direction: background bee-hazard framing with no pressure aimed at the biocide file.

### Tier B: indirect, hazard-based

#### 2025-10-15 — Dutch Bee Foundation lists indoxacarb among the nine pesticides behind nearly all the risk to bumblebee colonies and calls for less pesticide use and monitoring after approval
`PPP` · `national:NL` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`

- **Summary:** The Bijenstichting reports on a large European study of 316 bumblebee colonies in eight countries (published in Nature in April 2024). Pesticides were found in 95% of colonies, and colonies gained less weight and produced fewer bees and new queens. The article says the nine most dangerous substances accounted for almost all of the risk, and names "imidacloprid, indoxacarb, chlorpyrifos-ethyl, deltamethrin en dimethoaat" among them. The foundation calls for pesticide reduction (the Farm to Fork target of 50% by 2030), "pesticidovigilantie" (systematic monitoring after approval) and organic sourcing of bulbs and seeds.
- **Why it matters for the BPR renewal:** Indirect. The item targets agricultural pesticide policy, not biocides, and makes no specific demand about indoxacarb. It rests on hazard grounds (toxicity to bees), which carry over between regimes. But for PT18 indoxacarb, mostly enclosed gel and bait products used indoors, bee exposure is limited, so the effect on the BPR file is weak. It does add to the public picture of indoxacarb as a high-risk insecticide for pollinators.
- **NGO:** Bijenstichting (Dutch Bee Foundation): https://bijenstichting.nl/groot-europees-onderzoek-bevestigt-bestrijdingsmiddelen-blijven-hommels-zwaar-treffen/
