**2025-11-20: PAN Germany says permethrin is still used in biocidal wood preservatives and insecticides 25 years after being banned on crops, and calls for cutting pyrethroid use and emissions to surface waters, citing extreme toxicity to aquatic life** (TOP SIGNAL)
`water` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`
- Summary: PAN Germany reports on a 2025 scientific review (Hitzfeld et al., Environmental Sciences Europe) of synthetic pyrethroids in European surface waters. It singles out permethrin as an example of a substance banned in plant protection that is "still used as a biocide in wood preservatives and insecticides". It calls for reducing pyrethroid use and emissions to surface waters, better routine analytical capacity, harmonised pyrethroid rules and faster uptake of new science.
- Why it matters: this is the clearest NGO statement in the window linking permethrin's environmental burden to its biocide uses. It also lines up with the move to list permethrin as a Water Framework Directive priority substance with very low (pg-ng/L) quality standards. It rests on hazard grounds (aquatic toxicity), which carry over strongly into the BPR environmental assessment and the candidate-for-substitution debate. It is indirect because it targets water policy and pyrethroids as a group, but it names permethrin's biocide uses explicitly.
- NGO: PAN Germany, [Extrem toxisch für Wasserorganismen: Pyrethroid-Insektizide im Fokus von Wissenschaft und Regulation](https://pan-germany.org/pestizide/extrem-toxisch-fuer-wasserorganismen-pyrethroid-insektizide-im-fokus-von-wissenschaft-und-regulation/)

**2025-01-27: SongBird Survival-funded research finds pet flea and tick chemicals, including permethrin, in fur-lined bird nests, and the charity calls for a proper environmental risk assessment of veterinary drugs, citing more unhatched eggs and dead chicks**
`vet medicines` · `UK` · `indirect–use` · `study/report`
- Summary: SongBird Survival funded a University of Sussex study of 103 blue and great tit nests. Insecticides from pet treatments (fipronil, imidacloprid, permethrin) were found in the fur used to line them, and higher insecticide loads were linked to more unhatched eggs and dead chicks. The charity calls for "urgent action to ensure a greater environmental risk assessment of veterinary drugs".
- Why it matters: the exposure pathway (pet fur in nests) is specific to veterinary spot-ons and collars, so it carries over only weakly to the PT18 renewal. It does add to the evidence on secondary exposure from pet-applied permethrin, which also applies to PT18 products used on animal bedding or premises.
- NGO: SongBird Survival, [Veterinary drugs found in bird nests](https://www.songbird-survival.org.uk/post/veterinary-drugs-found-in-bird-nests)
