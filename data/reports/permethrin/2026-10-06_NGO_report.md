# NGO activity on Permethrin (CAS 52645-53-1): BPR PT18

## 1. Summary

Of the 4 top signals, 1 targets biocides directly (a Dutch campaign against consumer insecticides), 1 targets water policy, 1 is cross-regime exposure work tied to the Omnibus debate, and 1 targets veterinary medicines. No NGO has run an EU-level campaign, legal action or formal request aimed at the permethrin BPR renewal. NGOs bring permethrin up mainly as an example of a "banned on farms, still sold for homes and pets" insecticide. PAN Germany (November 2025, writing about a 2025 review of pyrethroids in European surface waters) and PAN Netherlands (April 2025 blacklist of household and garden insecticides) make this point most directly about biocide uses. PAN Europe's July 2026 blog on the SPRINT wristband study highlights very high permethrin levels in people's non-food exposure. The large UK flea-treatment campaign (WildFish, SongBird Survival, Wildlife and Countryside Link, 2026) now focuses on fipronil and imidacloprid and no longer names permethrin. Overall the pressure is low and spread out, but its direction (aquatic toxicity, endocrine and cancer concerns, consumer access) matches the hazard issues already open in the renewal.

## 2. Top signals

### Tier A: direct

**2025-04-07: PAN Netherlands puts permethrin on a blacklist of 10 insecticides sold to households and gardeners, and wants harmful pesticides kept out of non-professional hands, citing hormone-disrupting and neurotoxic effects**
`BPR` · `national:NL` · `direct` · `campaign`
- Summary: PAN-NL surveyed garden centres (Welkoop, Intratuin, Praxis, Hornbach and others) and online shops. It published a "zwarte lijst" of 10 insecticides it says should not be sold to private users: deltamethrin, flupyradifurone, imidacloprid, lambda-cyhalothrin, permethrin, prallethrin, pyrethrin, spinosad, tetramethrin and transfluthrin. It argues that some of these are banned in farming but still allowed for amateur use. The findings were broadcast on the consumer programme RADAR (NPO2).
- Why it matters: in the EU, permethrin's only consumer route is biocidal products (plus vet medicines), so this directly targets PT18 product authorisations for the general public. The campaign is national and aimed at retailers and consumers, not at the active-substance renewal. It rests mainly on hazard grounds (ED, neurotoxicity), which carry over strongly. It could support restrictions on consumer use at product-authorisation stage in the Netherlands.
- NGO: PAN Netherlands, [research report (PDF)](https://www.pan-netherlands.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/onderzoeksrapport.pdf), [press release (PDF)](https://www.pan-netherlands.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/persbericht.pdf). Partly verified: both PDFs open but their text could not be extracted. That permethrin is on the list comes from the search-index text of the PAN-NL report. Press coverage dated 2025-04-07 confirms that permethrin was named among the products on shelves. The PAN-NL blog post for the campaign now returns 404.

### Tier B: indirect, hazard-based

**2025-11-20: PAN Germany says permethrin is still used in biocidal wood preservatives and insecticides 25 years after being banned on crops, and calls for cutting pyrethroid use and emissions to surface waters, citing extreme toxicity to aquatic life** (TOP SIGNAL)
`water` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`
- Summary: PAN Germany reports on a 2025 scientific review (Hitzfeld et al., Environmental Sciences Europe) of synthetic pyrethroids in European surface waters. It singles out permethrin as an example of a substance banned in plant protection that is "still used as a biocide in wood preservatives and insecticides". It calls for reducing pyrethroid use and emissions to surface waters, better routine analytical capacity, harmonised pyrethroid rules and faster uptake of new science.
- Why it matters: this is the clearest NGO statement in the window linking permethrin's environmental burden to its biocide uses. It also lines up with the move to list permethrin as a Water Framework Directive priority substance with very low (pg-ng/L) quality standards. It rests on hazard grounds (aquatic toxicity), which carry over strongly into the BPR environmental assessment and the candidate-for-substitution debate. It is indirect because it targets water policy and pyrethroids as a group, but it names permethrin's biocide uses explicitly.
- NGO: PAN Germany, [Extrem toxisch für Wasserorganismen: Pyrethroid-Insektizide im Fokus von Wissenschaft und Regulation](https://pan-germany.org/pestizide/extrem-toxisch-fuer-wasserorganismen-pyrethroid-insektizide-im-fokus-von-wissenschaft-und-regulation/)

**2026-07-16: PAN Europe points to permethrin as one of the most common and highest-measured insecticides on wristbands worn by farmers and citizens, and asks the EU to enforce pesticide law instead of weakening it, citing widespread non-food exposure to toxic pesticide mixtures**
`cross-regime` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`
- Summary: PAN Europe's blog summarises the SPRINT wristband study: 641 people in 10 EU countries wore silicone wristbands in 2021. It notes that permethrin, "a neurotoxic pyrethroid" banned for pesticide use since 2003, was among the most detected insecticides, at up to 47,214 µg/kg. PAN Europe uses the findings to oppose the Commission's "food and feed safety" Omnibus and to call for cumulative risk assessment.
- Why it matters: since permethrin has no farm use, the exposure PAN Europe highlights most likely comes from biocidal and veterinary sources, though PAN Europe does not say this. The concern is hazard-based (neurotoxicity) plus real-world mixture exposure, which carries over to the BPR human-exposure assessment. It is indirect: PAN Europe aims it at PPP policy and the Omnibus, not at the biocide file.
- NGO: PAN Europe, [Wristband study proves: farmers and citizens exposed to highly toxic pesticides](https://www.pan-europe.info/blog/wristband-study-proves-farmers-and-citizens-exposed-highly-toxic-pesticides)

### Tier C: indirect, use-specific

**2025-01-27: SongBird Survival-funded research finds pet flea and tick chemicals, including permethrin, in fur-lined bird nests, and the charity calls for a proper environmental risk assessment of veterinary drugs, citing more unhatched eggs and dead chicks**
`vet medicines` · `UK` · `indirect–use` · `study/report`
- Summary: SongBird Survival funded a University of Sussex study of 103 blue and great tit nests. Insecticides from pet treatments (fipronil, imidacloprid, permethrin) were found in the fur used to line them, and higher insecticide loads were linked to more unhatched eggs and dead chicks. The charity calls for "urgent action to ensure a greater environmental risk assessment of veterinary drugs".
- Why it matters: the exposure pathway (pet fur in nests) is specific to veterinary spot-ons and collars, so it carries over only weakly to the PT18 renewal. It does add to the evidence on secondary exposure from pet-applied permethrin, which also applies to PT18 products used on animal bedding or premises.
- NGO: SongBird Survival, [Veterinary drugs found in bird nests](https://www.songbird-survival.org.uk/post/veterinary-drugs-found-in-bird-nests)

## 5. What NGOs say is coming next

- UK flea-treatment coalition (WildFish, SongBird Survival and others): pushing for over-the-counter spot-ons to become prescription-only and for river monitoring, after the 2026 House of Lords committee report. The focus is fipronil and imidacloprid, but any change to how products are sold would also cover permethrin vet products.
- PAN Germany: wants pyrethroid use and emissions reduced and pyrethroid rules harmonised across regimes. It describes permethrin as proposed for WFD priority-substance listing, which would set pg-ng/L quality standards relevant to biocide emission assessments.
- PAN Europe: campaigning against the "food and feed safety" Omnibus and for cumulative/mixture risk assessment, using the wristband data as evidence.
- PAN Netherlands: continuing to press retailers and the Dutch government to take high-hazard insecticides (including permethrin) off amateur shelves.
- No NGO has announced litigation, a formal request or a consultation response on the permethrin BPR renewal.
