# NGO activity report: Piperonyl butoxide (PBO), CAS 51-03-6

## 1. Summary

Of the 4 top signals, 1 targets biocidal products directly (Germany), 2 are food-residue findings and 1 is a cross-regime exposure study; none targets the EU biocide approval itself or calls for PBO to be banned. NGOs mostly mention PBO in passing, as one substance among many in testing and monitoring work. In June 2025 Verbraucherzentrale NRW and PAN Germany named PBO as a "problematic" ingredient in insect sprays in a market check of Germany's biocide sales rules. In December 2024 BUND called PBO "neurotoxic and a hormone poison", and Générations Futures found that PBO was the most frequently detected residue in French wheat, barley and rice. In July 2026 PAN Europe reported PBO among the most frequently detected substances on wristbands worn by people across 10 EU countries. The direction is low-level and hazard-flavoured (endocrine and neurotoxicity claims), with no campaign, legal action or formal request on PBO. The main background thread is PAN Europe's push on the EU review of pesticide synergists, which does not name PBO in the window.

## 2. Top signals

### Tier A: direct

**2025-06-11: Consumer and pesticide NGOs single out PBO in household insect sprays as problematic, citing possible harm to the hormone system and to nervous-system development**
`BPR` · `national:DE` · `direct` · `study/report`
- Summary: Verbraucherzentrale NRW and PAN Germany checked more than 130 shops in 17 NRW cities and Hamburg (March–April 2025). The check tested whether Germany's ban on self-service sales of insecticide sprays and its mandatory-advice rules (ChemBiozidDV) are being followed. They found self-service sprays gone from shelves but advice often inadequate. The Verbraucherzentrale release says: "Problematisch bei Insektensprays sind der Hilfsstoff PBO (Piperonylbutoxid) und die als Wirkstoffe eingesetzten Pyrethroide". It quotes its chemist saying there are indications some of these substances may harm the hormone system or nervous-system development. The organisations want stricter scrutiny of insect-killing actives, tighter enforcement and a complaints channel.
- Why it matters for the BPR renewal: This is the only item that targets biocidal products (PT18 insect sprays) directly. It rests on hazard grounds (possible endocrine and developmental neurotoxicity), which would carry over strongly if it were developed. However, it is a passing mention in a national sales-rules check, not a demand on PBO's EU approval. Note that the NGOs call PBO an auxiliary substance ("Hilfsstoff"), although it is an approved active substance under the BPR. Expect low direct impact unless the ED framing is picked up again.
- NGO: Verbraucherzentrale NRW, https://www.verbraucherzentrale.nrw/pressemitteilungen/insektenspray-beim-kauf-jetzt-besser-beraten-107953 ; PAN Germany (joint release), https://pan-germany.org/biozide/insektensprays-co-greifen-die-neuen-verkaufsregeln-im-handel/ (the PAN Germany page does not itself name PBO)

### Tier B: indirect, hazard-based

**2024-12-11: BUND labels PBO "neurotoxic and a hormone poison" after finding it in supermarket lentil chips, and calls for endocrine-disrupting pesticides to be banned outright**
`food residues` · `national:DE` · `indirect–hazard` · `study/report`
- Summary: In a ToxFox test, BUND found PBO at 0.011 mg/kg in "Funny frisch" lentil chips, below legal limits. In its results table it classifies PBO's human hazard as "Neurotoxisch und Hormongift". The factsheet demands a complete ban on pesticides that are especially hazardous, "z.B. endokrin wirksame Pestizide", plus a review of existing approvals where new studies exist and assessment of cocktail effects.
- Why it matters for the BPR renewal: This item is indirect but hazard-based. It publicly attaches an endocrine-disruptor label to PBO and links it to a general "ban EDs" demand, and an ED framing would carry over to the BPR file. It is weakened by being a single low-level residue in a broad consumer test with no PBO-specific follow-up.
- NGO: BUND, https://www.bund.net/service/publikationen/detail/publication/partyartikel-test-viele-schadstoffe/ (factsheet PDF: https://www.bund.net/fileadmin/user_upload_bund/publikationen/chemie/Partyartikel-Test-Viele-Schadstoffe-Factsheet-BUND.pdf)

### Tier C: indirect, use-specific

**2026-07-16: PAN Europe says PBO is among the insecticides most often found on wristbands worn by farmers and residents, and asks the EU to assess the combined and synergistic effects of such mixtures instead of weakening protections through the Omnibus**
`cross-regime` · `EU` · `indirect–use` · `study/report`
- Summary: PAN Europe's blog presents an EU-funded (SPRINT) study in which 641 people in 10 countries wore silicone wristbands for a week in 2021. 173 pesticides were detected. PBO is listed among the most detected insecticides across all groups, appearing together with permethrin, chlorpyrifos and DDT metabolites in over 70% of wristbands. PAN Europe asks the EU to "assess possible additive and synergistic effects of prevalent mixtures", to tighten regulation, and not to weaken protection through the Omnibus proposal.
- Why it matters for the BPR renewal: This is the most recent signal. Non-dietary exposure to PBO plausibly includes household and veterinary uses, which is close to biocides. However, the item rests on exposure and mixture grounds rather than an intrinsic hazard finding for PBO, and its demands target pesticide risk assessment generally, so it carries over weakly. It could feed a future argument that PBO's synergistic role should be considered in mixture assessments.
- NGO: PAN Europe, https://www.pan-europe.info/blog/wristband-study-proves-farmers-and-citizens-exposed-highly-toxic-pesticides

**2024-12-17: Générations Futures reports PBO as the most frequently detected residue in French wheat, barley, rice and split peas, and asks for faster phase-out of hazardous pesticides**
`food residues` · `national:FR` · `indirect–use` · `study/report`
- Summary: Générations Futures analysed official 2022 French monitoring data (1,996 non-organic samples). Its Annex II lists PBO as the top residue in wheat (31% of samples), barley (31%), rice (30%) and split peas (19%). PBO is given no CMR or ED classification in the report and its EU status is shown as "Pas encore évalué". The report's demands are general: support organic food, relaunch Ecophyto, and accelerate the exit of CMR, ED and PFAS pesticides.
- Why it matters for the BPR renewal: This is indirect and exposure-based. It documents widespread dietary residues of PBO (most likely from post-harvest grain storage use) without attaching a hazard claim, so it carries over weakly. It could become relevant if PBO is later framed as an ED, since the residue data already exist in NGO materials.
- NGO: Générations Futures, https://www.generations-futures.fr/actualites/residus-pesticides-vegetaux/ (report: https://www.generations-futures.fr/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/rapport-residu-vf.pdf)

## 5. What NGOs say is coming next

- Synergist review: PAN Europe is tracking the EU work programme for reviewing synergists under Reg. (EU) 2024/1487. It wants a shorter timeline, MRLs and groundwater limits for synergists, and withdrawal of products containing synergists that are excluded from the programme. PBO's PPP status is shown as "not yet evaluated" in Générations Futures' report, so the synergist review is the most likely next venue for NGO comment on PBO hazard data.
- Omnibus: PAN Europe (July 2026) is campaigning against the Commission's Omnibus simplification of pesticide rules and wants mixture and synergistic effects built into risk assessment. Because PBO is a synergist, this may bring PBO into the argument.
- Endocrine disruptors: BUND demands a complete ban on endocrine-active pesticides. PAN International notes that the new EU CLP hazard classes for endocrine disruptors become binding in November 2026 (PAN's description). An ED classification debate is the main route by which these items could move toward the BPR file.
- German biocide sales rules: Verbraucherzentrale NRW and PAN Germany call for stricter enforcement of the ChemBiozidDV, closure of the "repellent" relabelling loophole, and stricter scrutiny of insect-killing actives in sprays.
- Sherpa Duo: DUH describes its Sherpa Duo intervention (Braunschweig administrative court) as ongoing. No in-window update was found.
