# NGO activity on Tetramethrin (CAS 7696-12-0), 2024-10-06 to 2026-10-06

## 1. Summary

The one top signal targets the biocide regime directly, at national level in the Netherlands; no NGO item targets the EU BPR approval of tetramethrin. In April 2025 PAN Netherlands (PAN-NL) put tetramethrin on a "black list" of 10 insecticides sold for home and garden use. It wants these substances banned for non-professional users, citing hormone disruption, neurotoxicity and toxicity to insects and aquatic life. PAN-NL released the report alongside a broadcast on the Dutch consumer TV programme RADAR. Beyond that, NGO activity is at the level of the pyrethroid class. For example, Générations Futures (April 2025) wants all pyrethroid products withdrawn, including biocides, and PAN Germany (November 2025) wants pyrethroid emissions to water reduced. Neither names tetramethrin. Nothing turned up in the last 6 months (from 2026-04-06) that names tetramethrin and could be verified. Overall the direction is mildly negative, but the pressure is low and mostly about pyrethroids as a group.

## 2. Top signals

### Tier A: direct

**2025-04-07: PAN Netherlands wants tetramethrin and 9 other insecticides banned from consumer pest-control products, citing hormone-disrupting, neurotoxic and ecotoxic hazards**
`BPR` · `national:NL` · `direct` · `study/report`

- **Summary:** PAN-NL surveyed insecticides sold to consumers online and in garden centres, such as ant baits, wasp sprays and powders. Its report "PAN-NL zet 10 insecticiden op de zwarte lijst" names 10 substances, including tetramethrin, that it says should at minimum be banned for private, non-professional use: deltamethrin, flupyradifurone, imidacloprid, lambda-cyhalothrin, permethrin, prallethrin, pyrethrin, spinosad, tetramethrin and transfluthrin. The list is aimed at retailers, garden centres and citizens. PAN-NL notes that some of these substances are banned in agriculture but still allowed in consumer products.
- **Why it matters for the BPR approval:** It acts directly, because it targets biocidal products containing tetramethrin. Its reasons are hazard-based: endocrine disruption, neurotoxicity, reproductive toxicity and toxicity to insects and aquatic life. These line up with the open ED assessment for tetramethrin under the BPR and could feed into comparative assessment or restrictions on consumer use at product-authorisation stage (Ctgb). It is a national campaign that does not challenge the EU active-substance approval itself, and no follow-up naming tetramethrin was found. Impact is low to moderate.
- **NGO / link:** PAN Netherlands, research report (PDF): https://www.pan-netherlands.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/onderzoeksrapport.pdf ; press release (PDF, dated 2025-04-07): https://www.pan-netherlands.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/persbericht.pdf

## 5. What NGOs say is coming next

- PAN-NL wants harmful insecticides, tetramethrin included, banned for non-professional users. It has not announced a specific legal step or deadline on tetramethrin. Its separate legal actions before the Ctgb (a PFAS-pesticides case; a withdrawal request on deltamethrin) show it is willing to escalate on pyrethroids.
- PAN Europe and other NGOs are engaged in the European Commission's evaluation of the BPR (public consultation closed 2026-03-05) and in fighting the food/feed and pesticide "Omnibus" simplification. These are horizontal campaigns that could shape how ED-flagged biocides such as tetramethrin are handled, but none names tetramethrin.
- Générations Futures continues to push for all pyrethroids, biocidal uses included, to be withdrawn in France.
