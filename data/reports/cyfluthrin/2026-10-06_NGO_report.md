## 1. Summary

Both top signals target cyfluthrin's status as a pesticide that is banned in the EU but still exported. None target the biocide approval directly. In the past two years NGOs have named cyfluthrin by name only in the export campaign. The Konzernatlas 2026 (Heinrich Böll Foundation, BUND and the Dachverband Kritische Aktionärinnen und Aktionäre, January 2026) and its EU English edition, the Agribusiness Atlas (September 2026), cite Bayer's exports of cyfluthrin as an example of an "acutely toxic" EU-banned insecticide. They ask for an EU-wide export ban. PAN Europe and the End Toxic Pesticide Trade Coalition also criticised the Commission in June 2026 for dropping that promise, but without naming cyfluthrin. Wider NGO pressure on pyrethroids is growing, including in biocides. Générations Futures (FR) has demanded that all pyrethroid products, biocides included, be withdrawn. PAN-NL has blacklisted household insecticides, and PAN Germany has published on pyrethroids in water. None of these name cyfluthrin. Overall direction: little attention to this substance, but class-level pressure on pyrethroids is rising, and that could reach the PT18 renewal (expiry 2028).

### Tier B: indirect, hazard-based

- **Date:** 2026-09-30
  **Title:** Heinrich Böll Foundation calls for an EU-wide ban on exporting pesticides banned in the EU, naming Bayer's exports of cyfluthrin as an example of an insecticide that is "acutely toxic even at low doses"
  `PPP` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `campaign`
  **Summary:** The pesticides chapter of the Agribusiness Atlas 2026, published by the Böll Foundation's EU office (authors Silke Bollmohr and Susan Haffmans), describes how BASF, Bayer and Alzchem export pesticides that the EU has banned. It names cyfluthrin as Bayer's acutely toxic export product. It calls an EU-wide export ban "all the more urgent".
  **Why it matters for BPR:** Indirect. The target is the export of a substance that is no longer approved as a plant protection product. The cited concern is intrinsic acute toxicity, a hazard argument that carries over to any regime. NGOs who point out that cyfluthrin is "banned in the EU" while it stays approved for PT18 could draw attention to the biocide approval. For now, though, nobody is asking for any action on the biocide.
  **NGO / link:** Heinrich-Böll-Stiftung (EU office), https://eu.boell.org/en/2026/09/30/agribusinessatlas-pesticides-exports-despite-ban

- **Date:** 2026-01-07
  **Title:** German NGOs call for a Europe-wide export stop for EU-banned pesticides, singling out Bayer's exports of the acutely toxic insecticide cyfluthrin
  `PPP` · `national:DE` · `indirect–hazard` · `campaign`
  **Summary:** The Konzernatlas 2026 was co-published by the Heinrich Böll Foundation, BUND and the Dachverband Kritische Aktionärinnen und Aktionäre. Its pesticide chapter says German companies exported almost 50,000 t of non-approved substances in 2024, and quotes "Bayer exportiert das bereits in geringer Dosierung akut toxisch wirkende Insektizid Cyfluthrin". It demands "einen europaweiten Exportstopp für bereits verbotene Substanzen". PAN Germany promoted the atlas on 2026-01-08, but its post does not name cyfluthrin.
  **Why it matters for BPR:** Indirect and on hazard grounds (acute toxicity). This is the German-language original of the item above. Germany is the evaluating Competent Authority (eCA) for the cyfluthrin biocide dossier, so German NGO framing of cyfluthrin as a banned, acutely toxic substance is the most relevant context for the renewal. It is still aimed at trade policy, not at the BPR.
  **NGO / link:** Heinrich-Böll-Stiftung / BUND / Kritische Aktionäre, https://www.boell.de/de/2026/01/07/pestizide-der-eu-verboten-von-konzernen-exportiert (atlas page: https://www.bund.net/service/publikationen/detail/publication/konzernatlas-daten-und-fakten-ueber-die-agrar-und-lebensmittelindustrie/)

No NGO has announced a submission on the cyfluthrin BPR renewal itself.
