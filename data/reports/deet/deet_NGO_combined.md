### Tier B: indirect, hazard-based

**2026-07-10 — Beyond Pesticides (US) questions whether DEET is safe and promotes alternatives, citing skin absorption, effects on a nervous-system enzyme and seizures in over-exposed children**
`BPR` · `national:US` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`

- **Summary:** This daily news blog post covers mosquito and insect control. It draws on the article "The science of staying bite-free: Balancing natural essential oils, DEET safety and permethrin". It says a large share of a DEET dose passes into the skin and some enters the bloodstream, and cites a 2009 study finding that DEET interferes with a nervous-system enzyme in insects and mammals. It also mentions rare cases of brain swelling, tremors and seizures in small children after repeated overapplication. It presents these points as reasons to use safer management and alternatives.
- **Why it matters for the BPR renewal:** The argument rests on hazard grounds (neurotoxicity, dermal absorption), and hazard arguments carry over strongly between regimes. But this is a US blog post aimed at US consumers and the US EPA. It does not mention the EU BPR, it makes no formal request, and no EU NGO has picked it up. The route to the EU file is indirect and weak. The points it raises (dermal absorption, AChE inhibition) are already in the published literature that the French CA will assess.
- NGO: Beyond Pesticides, [Studies and actions advance safer management and alternatives to…](https://beyondpesticides.org/dailynewsblog/2026/07/studies-and-actions-advance-safer-management-and-alternatives-to-toxic-mosquito-and-insect-control/)
