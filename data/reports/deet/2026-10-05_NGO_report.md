## 1. Summary

No European NGO ran a campaign, legal action, position paper or testing programme on DEET in the window. Nothing targets the BPR PT19 renewal. The only critical material comes from US NGO Beyond Pesticides. In 2025-2026 it mentioned DEET in blog posts about pesticide hazards: childhood cancer and pregnancy exposure, alternatives to repellents, and DEET found in clouds and urine. These posts are general and do not campaign against DEET by itself. French consumer NGO UFC-Que Choisir points the other way: in July 2026 it called DEET the most effective repellent and the one to prioritise where mosquitoes carry disease. Overall the signal is weak and mixed. Supportive public-health framing currently outweighs the criticism.

## 2. Top signals (ranked by likely impact on BPR renewal)

**Tier 1: none.** No NGO activity in the window aims directly at DEET's EU approval or renewal.

**Tier 2: indirect, low impact**

1. **2026-07-10. Beyond Pesticides: "Studies and Actions Advance Safer Management and Alternatives to Toxic Mosquito and Insect Control"**
   - Summary: A blog post on mosquito and insect repellents. It says that "long-standing and mounting scientific evidence" links DEET and permethrin to adverse effects, and it recommends essential-oil and other "least-toxic" alternatives. It does not call for any regulatory action.
   - Why it matters: Indirect. It is a US source with no EU regulatory request. It argues on hazard grounds, which carry over strongly, but it brings no new evidence. It could feed an alternatives or substitution argument if EU NGOs pick it up.
   - NGO: Beyond Pesticides. https://beyondpesticides.org/dailynewsblog/2026/07/studies-and-actions-advance-safer-management-and-alternatives-to-toxic-mosquito-and-insect-control/

2. **2026-06-16. Beyond Pesticides: "Review of 88 Epidemiologic Studies Links Pesticides to Pediatric Brain Tumors and Leukemia"**
   - Summary: The post reports on a 2026 scoping review in the International Journal of Cancer. Its "take-home message" is to avoid pesticide exposure during pregnancy, "especially indoor insecticides such as flea and tick products, including DEET". DEET is named, but the evidence it cites concerns household pesticide use in general, not DEET specifically.
   - Why it matters: Indirect, class-level, on hazard grounds (developmental exposure and childhood cancer). This is the most hazard-relevant item found. It could be raised in the human-health part of the France-led evaluation, especially for use on children and in pregnancy. It is weak because it does not attribute any effect to DEET specifically.
   - NGO: Beyond Pesticides. https://beyondpesticides.org/dailynewsblog/2026/06/review-of-88-epidemiologic-studies-links-pesticides-to-pediatric-brain-tumors-and-leukemia/

3. **2026-07-15. UFC-Que Choisir: "Les moustiques vont-ils s'habituer au répulsif le plus efficace ?" (counter-signal)**
   - Summary: The French consumer association reports a study showing that mosquitoes can be conditioned to tolerate DEET. It quotes the lead author saying this is very unlikely to happen in the field. It calls DEET the most effective and most cost-effective personal protection and says DEET should be prioritised where mosquitoes transmit serious diseases.
   - Why it matters: Indirect and supportive. A major EU consumer NGO, in the evaluating Member State (France), supports continued DEET use on efficacy and public-health grounds. Its June 2026 comparison of repellents (https://www.quechoisir.org/comparatif-repulsif-n459/) also lists 50% DEET products. This weakens any case for substitution.
   - NGO: UFC-Que Choisir. https://www.quechoisir.org/actualite-les-moustiques-vont-ils-s-habituer-au-repulsif-le-plus-efficace-n177358/
