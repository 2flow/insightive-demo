## 1. Summary

Both top signals concern DEET's use as an insect repellent (BPR PT19). Only one is a direct EU signal, and it comes from a consumer association that supports DEET. No item in the window targets PPP, REACH/CLP, water, food residues or vet medicines. No EU environmental or health NGO (PAN Europe, PAN Germany, Générations Futures, ClientEarth, HEAL, GLOBAL 2000, BUND and others) has published anything on DEET between 2024-10-05 and 2026-10-05. The only critical item is a US blog post by Beyond Pesticides (2026-07-10) on DEET's health concerns. It is not linked to any EU process. In the EU, consumer organisations still present DEET as the benchmark repellent. Overall, NGOs are not putting pressure on the BPR renewal (France is the evaluating CA).

## 2. Top signals (ranked by likely impact on the BPR renewal)

### 2026-07-10 — Beyond Pesticides (US) questions whether DEET is safe and promotes alternatives, citing skin absorption, effects on a nervous-system enzyme and seizures in over-exposed children
`BPR` · `national:US` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`

- **Summary:** This daily news blog post covers mosquito and insect control. It draws on the article "The science of staying bite-free: Balancing natural essential oils, DEET safety and permethrin". It says a large share of a DEET dose passes into the skin and some enters the bloodstream, and cites a 2009 study finding that DEET interferes with a nervous-system enzyme in insects and mammals. It also mentions rare cases of brain swelling, tremors and seizures in small children after repeated overapplication. It presents these points as reasons to use safer management and alternatives.
- **Why it matters for the BPR renewal:** The argument rests on hazard grounds (neurotoxicity, dermal absorption), and hazard arguments carry over strongly between regimes. But this is a US blog post aimed at US consumers and the US EPA. It does not mention the EU BPR, it makes no formal request, and no EU NGO has picked it up. The route to the EU file is indirect and weak. The points it raises (dermal absorption, AChE inhibition) are already in the published literature that the French CA will assess.
- **NGO / link:** Beyond Pesticides — https://beyondpesticides.org/dailynewsblog/2026/07/studies-and-actions-advance-safer-management-and-alternatives-to-toxic-mosquito-and-insect-control/ (URL and date confirmed; the fetch tool returned only the site navigation, so the article content comes from the search engine's indexed text of this page)

### 2026-06-04 — French consumer group UFC-Que Choisir tests DEET repellents against tiger mosquitoes and keeps them among the best-performing products
`BPR` · `national:FR` · `direct` · `study/report`

- **Summary:** Que Choisir updated its comparative lab test of skin repellents against *Aedes albopictus* on 2026-06-04 (48 products). DEET products from 15% to 50% are included. The test also scores potential short- and medium-term toxicity on skin. The detailed scores are paywalled. In earlier editions a 50% DEET product ranked first.
- **Why it matters for the BPR renewal:** This is direct NGO testing of DEET biocidal products in the evaluating CA's own country. It is a counter-signal: it supports DEET on efficacy and public-health grounds (tiger mosquito, dengue and chikungunya in France) and calls for no restriction. It does not add hazard pressure. If anything, it backs the argument that DEET is essential for vector protection.
- **NGO / link:** UFC-Que Choisir — https://www.quechoisir.org/comparatif-repulsif-n459/
