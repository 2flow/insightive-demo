## 1. Summary

No European NGO has run a campaign, letter, legal action or testing programme aimed at DEET in the window. The only critical NGO material is from the US group Beyond Pesticides. It tags DEET in its daily news blog and, in July 2026, said that "mounting scientific evidence" links DEET to adverse health effects such as neurological symptoms and seizures, and pointed readers to essential-oil and other alternatives. In June 2026 it also passed on a review's advice to avoid indoor insecticides, "including DEET", during pregnancy. In Europe, the French consumer group UFC-Que Choisir runs the main NGO material, and it backs DEET: its 2026 repellent tests rate DEET products as the most effective, and in July 2026 it called DEET "the most effective individual protection tool and the least expensive". On balance, NGO pressure on the BPR renewal is low. The hazard-based criticism comes from outside the EU and does not target the BPR file, while EU consumer NGOs treat DEET as the benchmark repellent.

## 2. Top signals (ranked by likely impact on the BPR renewal)

**Tier 1: Hazard-based criticism (indirect, non-EU)**

1. **Date:** 2026-07-10
   - **Title:** US pesticide-reform NGO urges a switch from DEET to "least-toxic" repellents, citing evidence linking DEET to neurological symptoms and seizures
   - **Summary:** Beyond Pesticides' blog post on safer mosquito and insect control summarises an article on essential oils, DEET and permethrin. It says DEET is still widely used despite research linking it to adverse health effects, including neurological symptoms and seizures, and it promotes alternatives.
   - **Why it matters:** Indirect. This is a US NGO addressing US consumers and EPA. It makes no EU or BPR demand. The argument rests on hazard (neurotoxicity), which carries over strongly between regimes, but the post cites no new data. It could be picked up by EU NGOs or in public consultation comments, but there is no sign of that so far.
   - **NGO / link:** Beyond Pesticides, https://beyondpesticides.org/dailynewsblog/2026/07/studies-and-actions-advance-safer-management-and-alternatives-to-toxic-mosquito-and-insect-control/

2. **Date:** 2026-06-16
   - **Title:** US NGO passes on a review's advice that pregnant women avoid indoor insecticides "including DEET", citing links between pesticide exposure and childhood leukaemia and brain tumours
   - **Summary:** Beyond Pesticides reports on a review of 88 epidemiological studies (University of Nebraska Medical Center, published in the International Journal of Cancer) that links pesticide exposure to childhood cancers. Its key advice is to avoid pesticides in pregnancy, especially indoor insecticides such as flea and tick products, "including DEET".
   - **Why it matters:** Indirect and weak. The concern is about pesticides as a class, but DEET is named. It is a hazard and epidemiology argument, which could carry over, but the evidence is not specific to DEET. It touches the household and pet exposure route that is relevant to PT19 consumer use.
   - **NGO / link:** Beyond Pesticides, https://beyondpesticides.org/dailynewsblog/2026/06/review-of-88-epidemiologic-studies-links-pesticides-to-pediatric-brain-tumors-and-leukemia/

**Tier 2: Supportive NGO positions (reduce pressure)**

3. **Date:** 2026-07-15
   - **Title:** French consumer NGO says DEET remains the most effective and cheapest protection against mosquito-borne disease, and plays down fears that mosquitoes will become resistant
   - **Summary:** UFC-Que Choisir reports a French laboratory study showing mosquitoes can be conditioned to tolerate DEET. It quotes the researchers saying adaptation in the field is very unlikely and that DEET remains "the most effective individual protection tool and the least expensive". It raises no health or environmental concerns.
   - **Why it matters:** Indirect and supportive. A large EU consumer NGO publicly backs DEET's efficacy and public-health value. This works against any substitution or restriction narrative in France, which is the evaluating Member State.
   - **NGO / link:** UFC-Que Choisir, https://www.quechoisir.org/actualite-les-moustiques-vont-ils-s-habituer-au-repulsif-le-plus-efficace-n177358/

4. **Date:** 2026-06-04 (last update)
   - **Title:** French consumer NGO's repellent test ranks high-strength DEET sprays among the most effective against the tiger mosquito
   - **Summary:** UFC-Que Choisir's comparative test of skin repellents against *Aedes albopictus* includes several DEET products (30 to 50%). The page says potential short- and medium-term toxicity was also assessed. Detailed scores are behind a paywall.
   - **Why it matters:** Indirect and supportive. It is about efficacy and use. It strengthens the public-health case for keeping DEET in PT19 and does not raise hazard concerns.
   - **NGO / link:** UFC-Que Choisir, https://www.quechoisir.org/comparatif-repulsif-n459/
