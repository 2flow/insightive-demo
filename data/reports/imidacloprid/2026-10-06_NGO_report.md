# NGO activity report: imidacloprid (CAS 138261-41-3), BPR PT18

## 1. Summary

4 of 8 top signals target UK veterinary flea and tick medicines. 1 targets biocidal products directly (PAN Netherlands, April 2025). The rest cover water (Germany), EU food-import residues and the export of banned pesticides. Since February 2026 the strongest pressure has come from UK NGOs, led by Buglife, WildFish and SongBird Survival and backed by RSPB, The Wildlife Trusts, Friends of the Earth, Greenpeace UK and BTO. They argue that imidacloprid in pet spot-ons pollutes rivers above safe levels and is reaching songbird nests. They want these products made prescription-only, and some want them suspended. The NGOs welcomed a House of Lords committee report (published 2026-08-05) as backing their case. The only direct BPR signal is PAN Netherlands' consumer-product survey, which PAN Europe amplified. It put imidacloprid on a "black list" of insecticides in consumer ant, fly and cockroach baits and called for a ban on sales to non-professional users. The overall trend is a tightening, hazard-led story about aquatic toxicity, persistence and down-the-drain emissions. It is not yet aimed at the EU PT18 renewal itself, but it builds an evidence base that the evaluating competent authority (Germany) and a comparative assessment (imidacloprid is a candidate for substitution) could draw on.

## 2. Top signals

### Tier A: direct

**2025-04-08: PAN Netherlands and PAN Europe want toxic insecticides, including imidacloprid baits for ants, flies and cockroaches, banned from sale to amateur users, citing the toxicity of a "bee-killer" already banned in farming**
`BPR` · `national:NL` · `direct` · `study/report`
- Summary: PAN Netherlands surveyed home and garden insecticides sold online and in shops, alongside a parallel PAN Belgium survey. It published a "black list" of 10 insecticides, imidacloprid among them, and called for a ban on harmful pesticides for non-professional users. PAN Europe's write-up says that "the extremely toxic beekiller imidacloprid - banned in agriculture - is still available in pet products against fleas and to exterminate other insects." According to PAN Netherlands' report (as indexed by search), imidacloprid is sold to consumers as a biocide in baits for flies, garden ants, pharaoh ants and cockroaches.
- Why it matters: This is the only item found that targets imidacloprid biocidal products themselves. It works through national product authorisation, mainly amateur-use authorisations under the Ctgb, rather than the EU active-substance renewal. It mixes hazard grounds (neurotoxicity, bee toxicity), which carry over strongly, with use-specific grounds (untrained consumers), which carry over weakly. Because imidacloprid is a candidate for substitution, NGO arguments about alternatives and amateur-use restrictions could feed into comparative assessment of products after renewal.
- NGO: PAN Europe, [Highly toxic pesticides on sale for inexpert users](https://www.pan-europe.info/blog/highly-toxic-pesticides-sale-inexpert-users). Underlying report: PAN Netherlands, [PAN-NL zet 10 insecticiden op de zwarte lijst (PDF)](https://www.pan-netherlands.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/onderzoeksrapport.pdf). The PDF could not be text-extracted here, so its biocide-bait details come from the search index. The PAN Europe page was opened and confirmed.

### Tier B: indirect, hazard-based

**2026-06-11: PAN Germany says imidacloprid levels even in the North Sea are too high to meet future EU water-quality limits, and criticises weak federal action on pesticide and biocide inputs to water**
`water` · `national:DE` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`
- Summary: PAN Germany responded to the federal government's answers to a parliamentary question on water pollution. It notes that even in the North Sea, cypermethrin and imidacloprid concentrations are so high that the future environmental quality standards cannot be met. It also criticises the fact that biocide active substances are found in ground and surface water but are not monitored systematically.
- Why it matters: The case rests on hazard, since water-quality limits are derived from ecotoxicity, so it carries over strongly. It also explicitly links biocides to the water-monitoring gap. Germany is the evaluating competent authority for the imidacloprid PT18 renewal, so domestic NGO pressure on imidacloprid in water is relevant to how it handles environmental exposure in the assessment.
- NGO: PAN Germany, [Bundesregierung beantwortet Fragen zur Gewässerbelastung: Kaum Impulse gegen Pestizideinträge](https://pan-germany.org/wasser/bundesregierung-beantwortet-fragen-zur-gewaesserbelastung-kaum-impulse-gegen-pestizideintraege/)

**2025-12-16: Greenpeace's investigative unit maps EU exports of banned pesticides, naming imidacloprid as a "bee-killing" neonicotinoid still made in the EU for export**
`PPP` · `international` · `indirect–hazard` · `study/report`
- Summary: Unearthed (Greenpeace UK's investigations unit) published an interactive map of EU export notifications for banned pesticides. It names thiamethoxam and imidacloprid as "bee-killing 'neonicotinoids'" in the trade. It builds on the joint Unearthed/Public Eye investigation of September 2025 into growing exports of banned pesticides.
- Why it matters: The piece keeps imidacloprid's "banned bee-killer" label in public view. Its hazard framing (unacceptable risk to pollinators under PPP) reinforces the narrative that biocide approval is a loophole. It acts on the export and PPP regimes, not on BPR.
- NGO: Greenpeace / Unearthed, [Interactive map: the EU's banned pesticide trade](https://unearthed.greenpeace.org/2025/12/16/interactive-map-eu-banned-pesticide-trade/)

**2025-07-15: foodwatch finds imidacloprid among the banned pesticides most often found in EU food and calls for zero tolerance for such residues in imports, citing its classification as highly hazardous**
`food residues` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `campaign`
- Summary: foodwatch analysed EFSA's 2023 monitoring data. It reports that almost one in ten samples contained residues of pesticides no longer approved in the EU, and that imidacloprid was among the most frequently detected. It calls for an EU export ban and zero tolerance for residues of banned pesticides in imported food, backed by a petition with about 70,000 signatures.
- Why it matters: The campaign rests on hazard grounds (FAO highly-hazardous classification) and frames imidacloprid as banned in the EU. Its effect on BPR is indirect and modest: it adds to the general case against EU approvals of non-approved PPP substances, but it does not address biocidal uses.
- NGO: foodwatch, [Banned pesticides on EU plates](https://www.foodwatch.org/en/banned-pesticides-on-eu-plates) (German version, 2025-07-16: [Verbotene Pestizide landen auf unseren Tellern](https://www.foodwatch.org/de/verbotene-pestizide-landen-auf-unseren-tellern))

### Tier C: indirect, use-specific

**2026-08-05: SongBird Survival welcomes a House of Lords committee report backing curbs on imidacloprid and fipronil flea treatments, citing the chemicals found in 89% of tested tit nests**
`vet medicines` · `UK` · `indirect–use` · `statement`
- Summary: SongBird Survival welcomed the Lords Environment and Climate Change Committee report "Paws for concern: pets, parasites and pollution" and called it a turning point. It highlights University of Sussex research it funded, which found imidacloprid in 89% of tested blue tit and great tit nests and fipronil and imidacloprid in eggs and dead chicks. It calls for environmental impact assessments and prescription-only status.
- Why it matters: This is the highest-profile institutional endorsement of the NGO case so far, as the NGOs describe it. The demand is use-specific (pet spot-ons and down-the-drain emissions), so it carries over weakly to PT18 bait products. However, the wildlife-exposure and persistence evidence it popularises could be cited against any dispersive imidacloprid use.
- NGO: SongBird Survival, [House of Lords Committee call to restrict toxic pet pesticide treatments and fully assess environmental impact](https://www.songbird-survival.org.uk/post/house-of-lords-committee-call-to-restrict-toxic-pet-pesticide-treatments-and-fully-assess-environmental-impact)

**2026-04-24: WildFish pursues regulator records on imidacloprid and fipronil flea treatments and attacks the information watchdog's refusal, citing weak control of pet-medicine pollution**
`vet medicines` · `UK` · `indirect–use` · `formal request`
- Summary: WildFish asked the Veterinary Medicines Directorate (VMD) for information on imidacloprid and fipronil veterinary medicines from 2020 to 2025. The VMD said a reply would take 4,700 hours. WildFish complained to the Information Commissioner, who cut the estimate to 1,700 hours but upheld the refusal as "manifestly unreasonable". In its June 2025 legal update, WildFish had already reported legal correspondence with the VMD over its "failure… properly to control" these products and said broader recommendations would follow.
- Why it matters: This shows sustained legal and access-to-documents pressure on regulators over imidacloprid authorisations. The grounds are use-specific (veterinary authorisation and monitoring), and the action is UK-only, so carry-over to the EU BPR file is weak.
- NGO: WildFish, [Chemical pollution in rivers: latest updates on WildFish's legal work](https://wildfish.org/latest-news/latest-updates-on-wildfishs-legal-work/); earlier: [Legal Update - June 2025](https://wildfish.org/latest-news/legal-update-june-2025/)

**2026-04-21: Over 30 UK wildlife and veterinary groups ask the government to make imidacloprid and fipronil pet treatments prescription-only, citing river pollution above safe levels**
`vet medicines` · `UK` · `indirect–use` · `campaign`
- Summary: Buglife, WildFish and SongBird Survival coordinated an open letter to the Environment Minister. It was signed by 24 organisations (including RSPB, The Wildlife Trusts, Friends of the Earth, Greenpeace UK and BTO) and 11 academics. The letter asks for a timeline for the VMD review, prescription-only status, environmental quality standards for veterinary medicines, routine river monitoring and full environmental risk assessments. It notes that imidacloprid was identified as one of the highest-risk chemicals across the Greater London catchment and is linked to mayfly and caddisfly declines of over 90%.
- Why it matters: This is the broadest coalition action on imidacloprid in the window. It coincided with the VMD call for evidence (open 2026-04-16 to 2026-06-11). It is use-specific (pet products, wash-off to drains), but it strengthens the general case that any imidacloprid emission to wastewater is a problem. PT18 indoor baits also reach drains through cleaning, which an EU evaluator could pick up.
- NGO: Buglife, [Open letter, regarding veterinary parasiticides in the environment](https://www.buglife.org.uk/news/open-letter-veterinary-parasiticides-in-the-environment/); WildFish, [WildFish calls for government action on veterinary medicines](https://wildfish.org/latest-news/wildfish-joins-call-for-government-to-address-levels-of-flea-and-tick-treatment-chemicals-in-the-environment/); SongBird Survival, [Call for government to address levels of flea and tick treatment chemicals in the environment](https://www.songbird-survival.org.uk/post/call-for-government-to-address-levels-of-flea-and-tick-treatment-chemicals-in-the-environment)

**2026-02-20: Buglife report asks for imidacloprid and fipronil flea treatments to become prescription-only and for online sales to end, citing harm to river insects**
`vet medicines` · `UK` · `indirect–use` · `study/report`
- Summary: Buglife published "The effects of veterinary tick and flea treatments on freshwater invertebrates and ecosystems". It reports that imidacloprid posed the greatest risk across the Greater London catchment in 2023 and that 100% of wash-off samples from treated dogs contained imidacloprid and fipronil. It asks for prescription-only status, a ban on online sales, better environmental risk guidance and a move to safer treatments.
- Why it matters: This is the evidence base for the 2026 UK campaign. The findings are exposure-pathway specific (pet wash-off), so they carry over weakly to BPR. They do, however, document imidacloprid's aquatic toxicity and persistence, which are hazard properties.
- NGO: Buglife, [Love Your Pet, Not Pesticides: New Report Warns of Flea and Tick Treatment Threat to Rivers](https://www.buglife.org.uk/news/love-your-pet-not-pesticides-new-report-warns-of-flea-and-tick-treatment-threat-to-rivers/)

## 5. What NGOs say is coming next

- UK VMD review of imidacloprid and fipronil pet medicines: the call for evidence closed on 2026-06-11. The NGOs (Buglife, WildFish, SongBird Survival, Wildlife & Countryside Link) want a decision timeline and prescription-only status. Wildlife & Countryside Link goes further and seeks suspension of imidacloprid as a flea treatment (per its review; unverified).
- House of Lords committee report (2026-08-05): SongBird Survival calls it a "turning point" and is pressing the government to act on its recommendations.
- WildFish says it will examine veterinary medicine authorisation and monitoring more broadly and bring forward recommendations for regulatory change. It is also contesting regulator transparency after the Information Commissioner ruling.
- End Toxic Pesticide Trade coalition and foodwatch: continuing to push the Commission to deliver its 2020 pledge to ban exports of banned pesticides and to set zero tolerance for their residues in imports. This would cover imidacloprid as a non-approved PPP substance.
- PAN Netherlands: continuing its call for a ban on harmful insecticides, including imidacloprid baits, for non-professional users.
- No NGO was found announcing action aimed specifically at the EU PT18 imidacloprid renewal or at the ongoing CLH process.
