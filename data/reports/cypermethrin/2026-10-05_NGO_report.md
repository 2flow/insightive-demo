## 1. Summary

NGO activity on cypermethrin in the window is almost all about plant protection products (PPP) and is driven by PAN Europe's court case under the Aarhus Regulation. On 18 Dec 2025 the EU Court of Justice ruled, on appeal, that the Commission acted unlawfully when it re-approved cypermethrin in 2021. The court pointed to data gaps, risk mitigation for insects that had not been validated or was unrealistic, and no assessment of long-term toxicity of formulations. NGOs present this as the first successful civil-society challenge to an EU pesticide approval. In July 2026 the Commission answered by opening an Article 21 review rather than withdrawing the approval. PAN Europe calls this a delay and keeps calling cypermethrin a "bee-killer" and an endocrine disruptor. Beekeeping NGOs, PAN-NL, the Dutch Bee Foundation and ClientEarth (on transparency) have amplified the case, and several NGOs use it to argue against the Food and Feed Safety Omnibus. Biocide-specific activity is thin. PAN UK has flagged supermarket wasp and insect-killer biocides that contain cypermethrin, and PAN Germany has raised pyrethroid water pollution, including from biocide uses. The overall direction is rising pressure on the PPP approval on hazard grounds (endocrine disruption, genotoxicity of impurities, toxicity to bees and aquatic life). That hazard evidence is likely to feed into the BPR renewal (expiry 31/05/2030).

## 2. Top signals (ranked by likely impact on the BPR renewal)

### Tier 1: Hazard-based EU-level action that is likely to carry over

**2026-07-14: PAN Europe criticises the Commission for opening a lengthy review of cypermethrin instead of banning it after the EU court ruling, citing endocrine disruption, bee toxicity and missing long-term toxicity data**
- Summary: PAN Europe received the Commission's decision on its internal review request after the CJEU ruling. The Commission will start an Article 21 procedure (review of an approval in light of new scientific and technical evidence) under Reg. 1107/2009 and will not withdraw the approval now. PAN Europe calls this "unacceptable" (Martin Dermine) and asks for withdrawal, for long-term toxicity testing of formulations, and for stronger evidence requirements. PAN Europe's German-language page carried the same news on 17 Jul 2026 ("Kommission will Cypermethrin überprüfen").
- Why it matters for BPR: Indirect but strong. An Article 21 review re-opens the hazard profile of the active substance itself (endocrine disruption, genotoxic impurities, long-term toxicity). Any conclusion such as a confirmed ED finding would carry over strongly to the PT18 renewal and could trigger exclusion or substitution arguments under BPR. It rests mainly on hazard grounds. The insect-mitigation and drift arguments are use-specific and carry over weakly.
- NGO: PAN Europe, https://www.pan-europe.info/press-releases/2026/07/european-commission-delays-ban-cypermethrin-despite-eu-court-judgement

**2025-12-18: PAN Europe wins at the EU's highest court, which (as PAN Europe describes it) ruled the 2021 re-approval of cypermethrin unlawful, citing data gaps, unrealistic risk mitigation and no long-term toxicity assessment**
- Summary: PAN Europe reports that the CJEU (C-316/24 P) set aside part of the General Court's 2024 judgment. It also annulled the Commission's rejection of PAN Europe's internal review request. The Commission must now review its decision, which PAN Europe says should lead to withdrawal. PAN Europe calls cypermethrin highly toxic to bees and aquatic organisms and a suspected endocrine disruptor. PAN Netherlands republished the news on 19 Dec 2025 and added that cypermethrin regularly exceeds Water Framework Directive standards in Dutch surface water. The Dutch Bee Foundation (Bijenstichting) also covered it on 18 Dec 2025.
- Why it matters for BPR: Indirect. It sets a legal precedent that the Commission must justify departing from EFSA's science and cannot rely on unvalidated mitigation measures. NGOs can use the same argument against BPR decisions that depart from ECHA/BPC opinions. The ruling's points on data gaps and genotoxicity concern the substance and carry over. Its mitigation points are PPP-specific.
- NGO: PAN Europe, https://www.pan-europe.info/press-releases/2025/12/eu-highest-court-declares-toxic-insecticide-cypermethrin-illegally-re ; PAN-NL, https://www.pan-netherlands.org/europese-hof-het-insecticide-cypermethrine-onrechtmatig-opnieuw-goedgekeurd/ ; Bijenstichting, https://bijenstichting.nl/europees-hof-toelating-giftig-insecticide-cypermethrin-was-onrechtmatig/

**2025-06-05 / 2025-07-03: PAN Europe publicises the Advocate General's view that the Commission unlawfully re-approved cypermethrin, citing ignored EFSA warnings, isomer data gaps and possible genotoxic impurities**
- Summary: PAN Europe reported that the Advocate General backed most of its arguments: unrealistic drift reduction (95-99%), missing long-term and carcinogenicity data on formulations, isomer data treated as merely "confirmatory", and genotoxic potential of impurities not ruled out. The July blog sets out these four failures.
- Why it matters for BPR: Indirect, hazard-based (genotoxicity of impurities, isomer toxicology). These points concern the technical active substance and can carry over to the BPR reference specification and the renewal dossier.
- NGO: PAN Europe, https://www.pan-europe.info/press-releases/2025/06/eu-court-advocate-general-considers-eu-re-approval-pesticide-cypermethrin ; https://www.pan-europe.info/blog/advocate-general-slams-commission%E2%80%99s-handling-cypermethrin-case

### Tier 2: Amplification, transparency and policy campaigns

**2026-05-22: PAN Europe uses the cypermethrin ruling to ask the Commission to reject incomplete dossiers and require long-term toxicity data on whole products, citing its court win**
- Summary: The blog presents the cypermethrin judgment as a lesson for regulators. It asks the Commission to reject incomplete dossiers, require toxicity assessment of all co-formulants, and enforce assessment deadlines. PAN Europe sent letters on the same theme to the Commission and EFSA on 13 May 2026; the PDFs could not be read.
- Why it matters for BPR: Indirect. These are general demands on dossier completeness and product-level toxicity. Similar demands could be made of BPR product authorisations. They rest on procedure and hazard grounds.
- NGO: PAN Europe, https://www.pan-europe.info/blog/eu-court-rulings-should-lead-better-regulation-pesticides

**2026-04-10: Beekeepers' network BeeLife cites the cypermethrin ruling to oppose the Omnibus simplification, citing loss of scientific oversight and transparency**
- Summary: BeeLife's post on the CJEU ruling stresses that cypermethrin is highly toxic to bees and that no realistic safe use was identified. It warns that the Food and Feed Omnibus would weaken oversight and public scrutiny. The date is shown on the page as "April 10" without a year; 2026 is inferred because the post discusses the Dec 2025 ruling.
- Why it matters for BPR: Indirect and mostly about policy. It adds to pollinator-NGO framing of cypermethrin as a priority bee-toxic substance.
- NGO: BeeLife, https://www.bee-life.eu/post/eu-court-rules-cypermethrin-re-approval-illegal

**2026-03-25: ClientEarth wins a General Court case against secrecy over Member State votes on renewing cypermethrin, citing citizens' right to know how pesticide decisions are made**
- Summary: ClientEarth reports that the General Court found the lack of transparency in the comitology (Standing Committee) procedure unlawful, in a case that began with its 2021 request for documents on the cypermethrin renewal vote. ClientEarth asks policymakers to comply and improve transparency.
- Why it matters for BPR: Indirect and procedural. Standing Committee votes on biocides also go through comitology. More disclosure of Member State positions could raise public scrutiny of the BPR renewal vote. It does not rest on hazard grounds.
- NGO: ClientEarth, https://www.clientearth.org/latest/press-office/press-releases/secrecy-in-eu-pesticide-approvals-ruled-unlawful-a-landmark-win-for-transparency-and-nature

**2026-01-16: German beekeeping associations ask for an immediate end to approvals of hormone-disrupting pesticides and a halt to the Omnibus, citing the cypermethrin ruling and its nerve and hormone effects**
- Summary: Deutscher Berufs- und Erwerbsimkerbund (DBIB) and Imkerverband Rheinland-Pfalz call the CJEU ruling a turning point. They ask for no approvals without current risk assessment, assessment of cocktail effects, and immediate refusal of substances that affect reproduction or hormones. The source is their press release on presseportal (distribution service for the NGOs' own release).
- Why it matters for BPR: Indirect, hazard-based (ED). It adds to the endocrine-disruption narrative around cypermethrin.
- NGO: DBIB / Imkerverband Rheinland-Pfalz, https://www.presseportal.de/pm/59392/6197701

### Tier 3: Biocide- and water-specific signals (direct relevance, low intensity)

**2025-11-20: PAN Germany asks for lower pyrethroid use, including in biocides, and group-wide water quality standards, citing extreme toxicity to aquatic life (cypermethrin named as a Water Framework Directive priority substance)**
- Summary: PAN Germany presents a study on pyrethroids in water. It names cypermethrin as a WFD priority substance with limited validated monitoring data. It notes that pyrethroids are used as biocides in pest control and wood protection. It asks for lower use and emissions, better analytical capacity, harmonised regulation, public data on sales and use, and sum-based environmental quality standards for pyrethroids.
- Why it matters for BPR: Direct but class-level. It targets biocidal uses of pyrethroids and their emissions to water, which bear on the BPR environmental risk assessment for PT18. Aquatic toxicity is a hazard ground; the emission routes are use-specific.
- NGO: PAN Germany, https://pan-germany.org/pestizide/extrem-toxisch-fuer-wasserorganismen-pyrethroid-insektizide-im-fokus-von-wissenschaft-und-regulation/

**2024-11-13: PAN UK flags that Waitrose still sells wasp and insect-killer biocides containing cypermethrin, a Highly Hazardous Pesticide, in its supermarket ranking**
- Summary: PAN UK's 2024 Supermarket Ranking notes that Waitrose sells two biocidal products (wasp and insect killers) containing HHPs "such as cypermethrin". PAN UK's ranking materials say the company will look at replacing them with less harmful alternatives.
- Why it matters for BPR: Direct, on retail PT18 products. It acts through market pressure and could reduce downstream demand. It concerns UK (GB) retail rather than EU law and rests on hazard (HHP list) grounds. Its impact on the EU renewal is low.
- NGO: PAN UK, https://www.pan-uk.org/supermarkets/waitrose/

## 5. What NGOs say is coming next

- Article 21 review (PPP): PAN Europe expects a "lengthy" Commission procedure and keeps asking for withdrawal. No deadline has been given. NGOs are likely to submit evidence and may challenge delays. Watch for an EFSA mandate and outputs, which would update the hazard picture (ED, genotoxicity) relevant to the 2030 BPR renewal.
- Omnibus: PAN Europe, BeeLife and German beekeepers are campaigning against the Food and Feed Safety Omnibus. They use the cypermethrin ruling as their lead example of why periodic re-assessment must stay. BeeLife (Sep 2026 posts) describes the debate as entering a "decisive phase".
- Transparency: ClientEarth says it is pursuing further cases against the Commission's removal of transparency rules. These cases could affect how comitology votes, including biocide votes, are disclosed.
- Netherlands: PAN-NL had announced a CBb hearing on 11 March 2026 on Ctgb's refusal to withdraw cypermethrin products. No outcome was found on PAN-NL's site as of this run.
- Belgian derogations: PAN Europe and Nature & Progrès are actively suing Belgium over derogations (spirotetramat, June 2026). The reported 2026 Belgian derogation for cypermethrin (unverified) could become a target.
