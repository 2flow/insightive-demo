# NGO activity on Cypermethrin (CAS 52315-07-8), 2024-10-05 to 2026-10-05

## 1. Summary

5 of 7 top signals target the EU plant protection (PPP) approval; 1 targets biocides directly (a Générations Futures statement on biocide regulation and the Omnibus) and 1 targets water. The main event is PAN Europe's win at the Court of Justice of the EU on 2025-12-18 (case C-316/24 P). Earlier, in June 2025, the Advocate General had given an opinion in PAN Europe's favour. The Court annulled the Commission's rejection of PAN Europe's internal review request, as PAN Europe reports it. Since then PAN Europe and BeeLife have kept up the pressure: a World Bee Day letter to Commissioners Várhelyi and Roswall, a letter to DG SANTE, and in July 2026 an attack on the Commission's decision to open an Article 21 review instead of banning the substance outright. The arguments rest on hazard grounds (endocrine disruption, acute toxicity to bees and other non-target arthropods, aquatic toxicity, missing long-term toxicity data), and these carry over to the BPR file. Direct NGO attention to cypermethrin as a biocide is still thin. Générations Futures used cypermethrin as its example that only 75 of 905 declared biocidal products in France are fully authorised. The trend is rising and sustained on the PPP side. On the BPR side it is mostly indirect.

## 2. Top signals (ranked by likely impact on the BPR renewal)

### Tier A: direct

**2026-03-06. Générations Futures warns against weakening biocide rules under Omnibus X and against unlimited approvals, using cypermethrin to show that most biocidal products on the French market have never been fully evaluated**
`BPR` · `EU` · `direct` · `statement`
- Summary: In a piece on the limits of the biocides regulation, Générations Futures states that, according to France's biocid database, "sur 905 produits à base de cyperméthrine recensés, seuls 75 (8.3%) ont obtenu une AMM" (of 905 cypermethrin-based products listed, only 75, or 8.3%, have obtained a marketing authorisation). It opposes unlimited approvals for biocidal active substances under the Omnibus X proposal and calls for keeping periodic review, more resources for agencies and stricter industry dossiers. It also names pyrethroids as linked to behavioural disorders in children.
- Why it matters for BPR: Direct. This is the only item in the window that names cypermethrin in a biocides context. It argues about procedure and market surveillance, not new hazard data, but it makes cypermethrin the example product type for a French NGO campaign against Omnibus deregulation of the BPR. That makes renewal-stage scrutiny more likely.
- NGO: Générations Futures, https://www.generations-futures.fr/actualites/produit-biocides-generations-futures-pointe-les-limites-de-la-reglementation-actuelle-et-alerte-sur-les-risques-de-deregulation-dans-le-cadre-du-projet-domnibus-x/

### Tier B: indirect, hazard-based

**2026-07-14. PAN Europe accuses the Commission of stalling: it wants cypermethrin banned now rather than put through a lengthy new review, citing the court ruling and its bee-toxic and endocrine-disrupting properties**
`PPP` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`
- Summary: PAN Europe reports that, seven months after the judgment, the Commission chose to open an Article 21 procedure under Regulation 1107/2009 to look at new evidence on toxicity, instead of withdrawing the approval. Executive Director Martin Dermine called the reply "unacceptable". The release lists the open issues as no risk assessment of the representative formulations, gaps in genotoxicity data, unrealistic mitigation, and untested co-formulants.
- Why it matters for BPR: Indirect, on hazard grounds. An Article 21 review means fresh EU scrutiny of cypermethrin's hazard profile (genotoxicity, ED, non-target arthropods) in 2026–2027, just before the BPR renewal application window. Any adverse EFSA finding would flow into the biocide evaluation.
- NGO: PAN Europe, https://www.pan-europe.info/press-releases/2026/07/european-commission-delays-ban-cypermethrin-despite-eu-court-judgement

**2026-05-22 (letters dated 2026-05-13). PAN Europe writes to the Commission's food-safety directorate demanding it carry out the cypermethrin ruling and stop approving pesticides with no long-term toxicity assessment, citing the court's findings and a 2026 Belgian emergency derogation for cypermethrin uses banned since 2021**
`PPP` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`
- Summary: The letter is addressed to Deputy Director-General Claire Bury. It draws "lessons" from C-316/24 P: mitigation must be validated, batches must be representative, and long-term toxicity of the representative formulation "must be positively demonstrated". It invokes Article 266 TFEU to require compliance "within a reasonable time". It also complains that "in 2026, Belgium granted a derogation for uses of cypermethrin that have been banned since 2021" and asks whether DG SANTE will open infringement proceedings. A parallel letter went to EFSA the same day (not opened, see Method notes). A PAN Europe blog of 2026-05-22 sums up the demands.
- Why it matters for BPR: Indirect, on hazard grounds. It pushes for the formulation-level and long-term toxicity demands that would also apply to biocidal product dossiers, and it keeps cypermethrin as the named test case.
- NGO: PAN Europe, https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/Letters/20260513%20Letter%20to%20C.%20Bury_Lessons%20from%20recent%20EU%20verdicts%20on%20pesticides%201.pdf ; blog https://www.pan-europe.info/blog/eu-court-rulings-should-lead-better-regulation-pesticides

**2026-05-20. Bee NGOs ask the Commission to carry out the court ruling on cypermethrin at once, citing hormone disruption, acute toxicity to bees and EFSA's view that spray-drift mitigation cannot bring risks to safe levels**
`PPP` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`
- Summary: PAN Europe and BeeLife wrote to Commissioners Várhelyi and Roswall for World Bee Day, naming cypermethrin as one of three substances "requiring urgent regulatory action" alongside flupyradifurone and acetamiprid. They write: "We respectfully request that you immediately implement the Court ruling." They also use the Article 21 track record to oppose open-ended approvals under the Food and Feed Safety Omnibus. BeeLife publicised the letter on its site.
- Why it matters for BPR: Indirect, on hazard grounds. It brings the beekeeping sector in behind the cypermethrin case and ties it to the Omnibus fight over unlimited approvals, which also bears on biocide approvals.
- NGO: PAN Europe and BeeLife, https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/Letters/20260520%20World%20Bee%20Day%20Letter%20PAN%20Europe%20and%20BeeLife%20to%20Com.%20Roswall%20and%20Varhelyi.pdf ; https://www.bee-life.eu/post/joint-letter-to-the-european-commission-protecting-pollinators-requires-action

**2025-12-18. PAN Europe wins at the EU's highest court: the 2021 re-approval of cypermethrin as a pesticide was unlawful, citing gaps in the dossier, unrealistic risk-mitigation measures for insects and no assessment of long-term toxicity**
`PPP` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `litigation`
- Summary: PAN Europe reports that the Court of Justice set aside the General Court's February 2024 judgment and annulled the Commission's rejection of PAN Europe's internal review request against the 2021 renewal. PAN Europe says the Court found major gaps in the dossier, reliance on unscientific and unrealistic risk-mitigation measures for insects, and no evaluation of the long-term toxicity of at least one cypermethrin product. PAN Europe quotes EFSA that "no safe use of cypermethrin could be identified under realistic conditions", and says the Commission must now review and likely withdraw the approval. The Advocate General's opinion of 2025-06-05 had already backed PAN Europe (see section 3).
- Why it matters for BPR: This is indirect, but on hazard grounds, so it carries over strongly. It establishes on the record of the EU's highest court (as described by the NGO) that the EU-level case for cypermethrin's safety in agriculture is unresolved, with EFSA critical concerns on bees, aquatic organisms and endocrine disruption. Those hazard arguments, and the precedent that unvalidated mitigation cannot override critical concerns, are likely to be raised again at the BPR renewal (expiry 2030), especially with an ED evaluation and CLH proposal open.
- NGO: PAN Europe, https://www.pan-europe.info/press-releases/2025/12/eu-highest-court-declares-toxic-insecticide-cypermethrin-illegally-re

**2025-11-20. PAN Germany calls for less pyrethroid use and tighter, harmonised rules across pesticide and biocide uses, citing extreme toxicity to aquatic life and cypermethrin's status as a Water Framework Directive priority substance**
`water` · `national:DE` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`
- Summary: PAN Germany presents scientists' findings on pyrethroids in surface waters and notes that "Cypermethrin ist als prioritäre Substanz im Rahmen der Wasserrahmenrichtlinie (WRRL) gelistet" (cypermethrin is listed as a priority substance under the Water Framework Directive). It points out that pyrethroids are used both as agricultural pesticides and "als Biozide in Mitteln zur Schädlingsbekämpfung" (as biocides in pest-control products). It asks for lower emissions, group-based environmental quality standards for total pyrethroids, mixture-toxicity indicators and harmonised regulation.
- Why it matters for BPR: Indirect, on hazard grounds, and it explicitly links water contamination to biocidal uses. Group or mixture standards for pyrethroids would tighten the environmental risk assessment of biocidal emissions (e.g. via sewage from indoor insecticide use).
- NGO: PAN Germany, https://pan-germany.org/pestizide/extrem-toxisch-fuer-wasserorganismen-pyrethroid-insektizide-im-fokus-von-wissenschaft-und-regulation/

### Tier C: indirect, use-specific

**2026-03-25. ClientEarth wins a ruling that keeping Member States' votes on the cypermethrin renewal secret was unlawful, citing the public's right to know why the renewal went ahead despite EFSA's warnings on bees and aquatic life**
`PPP` · `EU` · `indirect–use` · `litigation`
- Summary: ClientEarth reports that the EU's top court ruled the lack of transparency in comitology decisions on pesticide authorisations unlawful. The case concerned its 2021 access-to-documents request on the cypermethrin renewal, and the release repeats EFSA's critical concerns (high risk to aquatic organisms, honeybees and off-field non-target arthropods). ClientEarth's lawyer said "decisions that affect people's health and the environment cannot be taken behind closed doors."
- Why it matters for BPR: Indirect and procedural rather than hazard-based, so it carries over weakly on substance. It could however open up Member State positions in comitology votes, including the Standing Committee on Biocidal Products, to public and NGO scrutiny at renewal time.
- NGO: ClientEarth, https://www.clientearth.org/latest/press-office/press-releases/secrecy-in-eu-pesticide-approvals-ruled-unlawful-a-landmark-win-for-transparency-and-nature

## 5. What NGOs say is coming next

- Article 21 review of the PPP approval: the Commission has opened an Article 21 procedure. PAN Europe calls this a delay and demands an immediate ban, citing Article 266 TFEU compliance "within a reasonable time" (2026-07-14; 2026-05-13 letter). Further NGO pressure, and possibly a new legal step over non-compliance, are likely while EFSA reassesses.
- National derogations: PAN Europe has asked DG SANTE whether it will open infringement proceedings over Belgium's 2026 emergency derogation for cypermethrin uses banned since 2021.
- Dutch litigation: PAN Netherlands and Milieufederatie Zuid-Holland's CBb appeal against Ctgb's refusal to withdraw cypermethrin products had a hearing set for 2026-03-11. No outcome was found.
- Omnibus fights: Générations Futures (biocides, Omnibus X) and PAN Europe and BeeLife (Food and Feed Safety Omnibus) oppose unlimited approvals and want periodic renewal kept. That bears directly on whether cypermethrin's BPR approval faces a full renewal in 2030.
- Pyrethroid water standards: PAN Germany is pushing for group and mixture-based quality standards for pyrethroids in surface waters, which would cover biocidal emissions.
