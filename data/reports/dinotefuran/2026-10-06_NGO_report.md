# NGO activity on Dinotefuran (CAS 165252-70-0), 2024-10-06 to 2026-10-06

## 1. Summary

None of the 3 top signals targets the biocide approval directly. 1 targets UK veterinary medicines and 2 are US NGO blog posts on pesticide regulation (PPP). In the EU, NGOs have said very little about dinotefuran specifically. It has never been approved as an EU plant protection product, so it does not appear in PAN Europe, BeeLife, ClientEarth or Générations Futures campaigns on neonicotinoids or the Omnibus. The most relevant European item is PAN UK's ongoing campaign (launched 2023-11-01 with an open letter co-signed by Buglife and The Wildlife Trusts; campaign page last updated 2024-10-22). It names dinotefuran as one of five "highly bee-toxic" substances that should be taken out of pet flea and tick products. In 2025–2026 the UK pet-parasiticide debate grew: the coalition letter of 2026-04-21 and the House of Lords committee report of 2026-08-05, as SongBird Survival describes it. However, that debate centres on fipronil and imidacloprid and does not name dinotefuran. In the US, Beyond Pesticides keeps publishing hazard material that names dinotefuran (bee thermoregulation, human biomonitoring). The overall direction is low, mostly class-level pressure, with no NGO action on the PT18 file.

## 2. Top signals

### Tier A: direct

### Tier B: indirect, hazard-based

**2026-01-29: Beyond Pesticides calls for neonicotinoids, including dinotefuran, to be phased out, citing a study showing dinotefuran makes honeybees overheat and speeds the carrying of contaminated nectar into hives**
`PPP` · `national:US` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`
- Summary: A Beyond Pesticides blog post reports a 2025 study in which dinotefuran raised octopamine levels and body temperature in honeybees and increased flight-gene expression. According to the study, this speeds up the transport of contaminated nectar into the hive. The S-enantiomer had the stronger effect. The post uses the study to argue for phasing out neonicotinoids and moving to organic management.
- Why it matters for the BPR renewal: This is an indirect, hazard-based signal. Hazard evidence on bee toxicity carries over between regimes. It adds to the published literature that a Candidate for Substitution comparative assessment or a later renewal could cite. Its weight is limited: it is a US advocacy blog with no EU ask, and PT18 indoor crack-and-crevice uses give little pollinator exposure.
- NGO: Beyond Pesticides, https://beyondpesticides.org/dailynewsblog/2026/01/neonicotinoid-insecticides-cause-deadly-overheating-behavior-in-honey-bees-study-finds/

**2024-10-22: PAN UK campaigns for a UK ban on crop-banned insecticides in pet flea and tick medicines, naming dinotefuran as one of five highly bee-toxic substances (campaign page last updated on this date)**
`vet medicines` · `UK` · `indirect–hazard` · `campaign`
- Summary: PAN UK's "Pesticides in veterinary medicines" campaign page (first published 2023-10-31, last modified 2024-10-22) lists dinotefuran with fipronil, imidacloprid, nitenpyram and permethrin as substances that are not permitted on agricultural crops but are used in pet medicines. It asks the UK Government to ban such substances from pet medicines and close the loophole for future crop-banned pesticides. It also asks for veterinary approvals to cover environmental risk, and for retailers to stop selling these products. The related open letter (2023-11-01, outside the window) describes dinotefuran as never approved for crops and "highly toxic to bees".
- Why it matters for the BPR renewal: This is indirect and rests mainly on hazard grounds: bee toxicity, and the fact that dinotefuran was never approved for crops. These points carry over well to a PT18 file that is already a Candidate for Substitution. It targets a non-agricultural, household-type use close to PT18 products, and it is the clearest European NGO statement naming dinotefuran. It is UK-only, the date reflects a page update rather than new activity, and later UK campaigning dropped dinotefuran to focus on fipronil and imidacloprid.
- NGO: PAN UK, https://www.pan-uk.org/pesticides-in-pet-meds/ (open letter: https://www.pan-uk.org/pet-meds-environment/)

### Tier C: indirect, use-specific

**2025-07-15: Beyond Pesticides calls on the US EPA to move households away from chemical pest control, citing a study that found dinotefuran among the five pesticides with the highest urine concentrations in Indiana households**
`PPP` · `national:US` · `indirect–use` · `statement`
- Summary: Beyond Pesticides reports an Indiana study of pesticides in indoor dust, drinking water and urine. It quotes the authors: "The top five highest concentrations of pesticides in urine samples included imidacloprid, dinotefuran, 2,4-D, Metolachlor OA, and 3-PBA". The post calls for a shift away from chemical pest control and for the EPA to meet its obligations under federal pesticide and wildlife law.
- Why it matters for the BPR renewal: This is indirect and rests on exposure grounds, so it carries over only weakly. Household biomonitoring is still relevant to indoor biocidal uses such as PT18, because it supports arguments about residential exposure. The post does not separate out indoor-use sources and makes no EU ask.
- NGO: Beyond Pesticides, https://beyondpesticides.org/dailynewsblog/2025/07/pesticides-persist-in-indoor-dust-drinking-water-and-urine-in-households-according-to-indiana-study/

## 5. What NGOs say is coming next

- UK: SongBird Survival, Buglife and WildFish are pressing the UK Government to respond to the House of Lords report of 2026-08-05 with prescription-only status, full environmental risk assessment and waterway monitoring for pet parasiticides. This could widen to other spot-on actives, but the coalition's asks currently name only fipronil and imidacloprid. PAN UK's standing ask to ban all crop-banned pesticides, including dinotefuran, from pet medicines remains open.
- EU: PAN Europe, Générations Futures and foodwatch are campaigning against the Food and Feed Safety Omnibus. Their next milestones are Council and Parliament negotiations during 2026 and an entry-into-force scenario on 2027-01-01. None of them has said anything about biocide approvals or dinotefuran.
- US: Beyond Pesticides continues to call for neonicotinoids to be phased out ahead of the EPA's registration review decisions on the neonicotinoids, including dinotefuran.
- No NGO has announced any action on the PT18 dinotefuran renewal, the EU CLH process or the Candidate for Substitution status.
