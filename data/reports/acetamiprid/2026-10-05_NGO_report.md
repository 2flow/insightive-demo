## 1. Summary

NGO pressure on acetamiprid is intense and growing, but most of it targets plant protection uses: the French "Loi Duplomb" fights (2025) and the "Loi d'urgence agricole" (2026), plus EU calls for an immediate ban. On the biocide file, the key item comes from Générations Futures (30 Sep 2025). It reports that the PT18 approval holder filed no renewal application by the July 2025 deadline, so the BPR approval will lapse on 31 Jan 2027. NGOs (Générations Futures, PAN Europe, BeeLife) also use the BPR dossier as evidence. They point to the biocide assessment's "very persistent" finding and its "candidate for substitution" status to attack the pesticide approval. Their hazard arguments are developmental neurotoxicity, endocrine disruption, a push for Repr. 1B classification, and harm to wild bees and birds. These carry over directly to any future biocide application. Overall direction: pushing toward a full EU ban across all regimes. A late-2026 flashpoint is the coming ANSES decisions on French derogations, which Générations Futures says it will challenge in court.

## 2. Top signals (ranked by likely impact on the BPR renewal)

### Tier 1: direct bearing on the BPR file

**30 Sep 2025: Générations Futures reports that the manufacturer filed no application to renew acetamiprid as a biocide, so household insecticides containing it must leave the EU market, and uses this to demand an agricultural ban too**
- Summary: Générations Futures reports that Nisso Chemical Europe filed no renewal request for acetamiprid as a biocide before the 31 Jul 2025 deadline, citing ECHA's renewal-interest list of 19 Sep 2025. On its account, the approval ends on 31 Jan 2027, household insecticides will disappear from 2027 and the substance will be fully banned from early 2028. Its toxicologist suggests industry knew re-approval had "no chance" given the pending ED and DNT data. The group calls for a total EU ban, including in agriculture.
- Why it matters: direct. This is an NGO's description of the BPR process, not an official source, so confirm it against ECHA. If accurate, there is no renewal to influence, and the BPR file closes on expiry. The hazard grounds it cites (ED, DNT) carry over strongly, which makes any later new application under BPR unlikely to succeed.
- NGO: Générations Futures. https://www.generations-futures.fr/actualites/acetamipride-biocide-duplomb/

**20 May 2026: Bee NGOs ask the EU for a new safety check on acetamiprid's risk to bees, including wild bees, and point out that the EU's own biocide assessment calls it very persistent**
- Summary: PAN Europe and BeeLife sent a World Bee Day letter to Commissioners Roswall and Várhelyi. It notes that the biocide dossier calls acetamiprid "very persistent in the environment" while the pesticide dossier calls it non-persistent, and says this "warrants urgent scrutiny." The letter welcomes the Article 21 review (DNT and ED) mandated to EFSA in May 2025, as the NGOs describe it. It asks for a separate EFSA mandate on bees and other non-target arthropods under the 2023 Bee Guidance Document. It also uses acetamiprid as an example of why the Omnibus "open-ended approval" proposal should be dropped.
- Why it matters: indirect but explicit. It uses the BPR persistence finding as a lever on the PPP regime. Persistence is a hazard property, so the argument carries over strongly. It also reinforces the existing candidate-for-substitution status.
- NGO: PAN Europe and BeeLife. https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/Letters/20260520%20World%20Bee%20Day%20Letter%20PAN%20Europe%20and%20BeeLife%20to%20Com.%20Roswall%20and%20Varhelyi.pdf ; BeeLife summary page: https://www.bee-life.eu/post/joint-letter-to-the-european-commission-protecting-pollinators-requires-action

**20–22 May 2025: Générations Futures and PAN Europe ask the EU to suspend and ban acetamiprid at once and to classify it as toxic to reproduction (Cat. 1B), citing 16 new studies on harm to the developing brain**
- Summary: The two NGOs wrote to the Commission (DG SANTE) presenting 16 peer-reviewed studies. They ask for immediate suspension of the approval, Repr. 1B classification and a full ban. They note that EFSA has asked for a DNT study since 2013 without industry providing one. They cite the PT18 approval running to 2027 and the ECHA competent-authority report's "very persistent" finding. Générations Futures' companion report (20 May 2025) asks for an EU-wide ban covering both pesticide and biocide uses. It also asks France to extend its agricultural ban to biocides, citing risks to professional users and children from biocide exposure.
- Why it matters: direct and hazard-based. A Repr. 1B classification would trigger BPR exclusion criteria (Art. 5), and the DNT and ED arguments carry over fully to the biocide regime. It explicitly names biocide uses.
- NGO: PAN Europe and Générations Futures. https://www.pan-europe.info/press-releases/2025/05/pan-europe-and-g%C3%A9n%C3%A9rations-futures-ask-european-commission-ban-neurotoxic ; https://www.generations-futures.fr/actualites/acetamipride-duplomb/

**22 May 2025: Générations Futures accuses France's agriculture minister of misleading the public about acetamiprid in household and pet products, saying 213 French biocide products contain it and none has been fully assessed**
- Summary: Générations Futures rebuts the minister's claim that acetamiprid is in "all" household insecticides and in flea collars. It says only 213 biocidal insecticides on the French market contain it, all still with "pending" or "transitional" status. It says no veterinary medicine in France contains acetamiprid. It calls for an EU ban on biocide and agricultural uses.
- Why it matters: direct, and about biocide product exposure (use-specific, so it carries over weakly). It shows NGOs are watching the biocide product portfolio closely.
- NGO: Générations Futures. https://www.generations-futures.fr/actualites/acetamipride-antipuce-genevard/

### Tier 2: indirect (PPP regime and policy), hazard-based

**14 Aug 2026: Générations Futures says it will sue if France's food safety agency approves new acetamiprid exemptions, after the Constitutional Council (as the NGO describes it) upheld the law allowing them**
- Summary: According to Générations Futures, the Constitutional Council upheld the derogation mechanism in the agricultural emergency law, with two interpretive reservations. For acetamiprid the derogation covers hazelnuts. ANSES must decide within two months, and silence counts as refusal. The group says it will "go to court" against any derogation granted.
- Why it matters: indirect (PPP, France). It keeps the hazard debate in public view, and litigation could produce new expert findings. It does not act on the BPR file.
- NGO: Générations Futures. https://www.generations-futures.fr/actualites/decision-conseil-constitutionnel-neonicotinoides/

**3–4 Aug 2026: Nine French NGOs ask the Constitutional Council to strike down the acetamiprid exemption, citing risks to biodiversity, water and human brain development**
- Summary: Générations Futures, POLLINIS, WWF France, LPO, Notre Affaire à Tous, WELFARM, Biodiversité sous nos pieds, Confédération paysanne and FNE filed external contributions to the Council. They argue that reintroducing acetamiprid breaches the precautionary and prevention principles of the Environmental Charter.
- Why it matters: indirect, hazard-based. It shows a broad coalition and legal mobilisation.
- NGOs: Générations Futures and partners, https://www.generations-futures.fr/actualites/urgence-agricole-contribution-conseil-constitutionnel/ ; POLLINIS, https://www.pollinis.org/publications/loi-durgence-agricole-pollinis-depose-une-contribution-devant-le-conseil-constitutionnel/

**17 Jun – 20 Jul 2026: French NGOs campaign against the Senate's revival of acetamiprid exemptions (the "Duplomb 2" provisions), citing harm to pollinators, birds and health and the precautionary principle**
- Summary: Générations Futures condemned the Senate committee's addition of the provisions to the emergency agricultural bill (17 Jun). It then condemned the 183–129 Senate vote (30 Jun) and argued that ANSES cannot properly assess the substances in two months (20 Jul). It cited a petition of more than 2 million signatures and the "Collectif Nourrir" coalition of over 50 organisations. POLLINIS ran a parallel campaign from 28 Apr 2026.
- Why it matters: indirect. It feeds public and political pressure, but its target is PPP derogations in France.
- NGOs: Générations Futures, https://www.generations-futures.fr/actualites/duplomb-2-senat/ , https://www.generations-futures.fr/actualites/alerte-neonicotinoides-duplomb/ , https://www.generations-futures.fr/actualites/evaluation-anses-neonicotinoides/ ; POLLINIS, https://www.pollinis.org/publications/projet-de-loi-durgence-agricole-pollinis-sinquiete-dune-reautorisation-des-neonicotinoides/

**5 Feb 2026 and 17 Mar 2026: PAN Europe and Générations Futures warn that the EU Omnibus plan would give acetamiprid an approval with no end date despite evidence of harm to the developing brain**
- Summary: Générations Futures' analysis lists acetamiprid among 49 pesticides that would get unlimited approval if the Food and Feed Safety Omnibus takes effect on 1 Jan 2027. It quotes the Commission's own estimate of €176,100 in savings from dropping acetamiprid's renewal. PAN Europe's March campaign ("For Health, Bees and Farmers") names acetamiprid again.
- Why it matters: indirect, policy. It concerns the PPP renewal architecture. It signals that NGOs will resist any easing of renewal rules that could later spread to biocides.
- NGO: PAN Europe and Générations Futures. https://www.pan-europe.info/press-releases/2026/02/pesticide-action-network-raises-alarm-over-49-pesticides-facing-unlimited ; https://www.pan-europe.info/press-releases/2026/03/health-bees-and-farmers-campaign-demand-better-not-weaker-pesticide-rules

**4 Dec 2025: Friends of the Earth Europe warns the Omnibus would let acetamiprid stay on the market indefinitely despite links to developmental brain harm**
- Summary: The publication calls acetamiprid "the last widely approved neonicotinoid in the EU linked to developmental brain harm." It argues against lifetime approvals and for keeping mandatory re-evaluation.
- Why it matters: indirect, policy and hazard framing.
- NGO: Friends of the Earth Europe. https://friendsoftheearth.eu/publication/europes-pesticide-protections-under-attack/

### Tier 3: monitoring and residue testing (exposure evidence)

**29 Jan 2026: PAN Europe network finds acetamiprid in nearly one in five European apples, calling it toxic to the foetal brain**
- Summary: The pan-European apple test covered 59 samples from 13 countries. PAN Europe and PAN Germany report acetamiprid in about 19% of samples. They demand stronger rules on mixture effects and rejection of deregulation.
- Why it matters: indirect. Dietary exposure has little bearing on biocide uses, but the testing sustains the hazard narrative.
- NGO: PAN Europe, https://www.pan-europe.info/press-releases/2026/01/european-apples-contaminated-cocktails-pesticides-pfas-neurotoxins-and-other ; PAN Germany, https://pan-germany.org/pestizide/europaeische-aepfel-haeufig-mit-pestizidgemischen-belastet/

**20 May 2026: GLOBAL 2000 finds acetamiprid among nine highly bee-toxic pesticides on "bee-friendly" garden plants and calls for a ban on such pesticides in home gardens**
- Summary: The Austrian test of ornamental plants found acetamiprid among 41 pesticides detected. Demands include ending synthetic pesticide use in home and garden settings.
- Why it matters: indirect. It concerns amateur and garden exposure, close to consumer biocide markets, but through the PPP route.
- NGO: GLOBAL 2000. https://www.global2000.at/publikationen/bienenfreundliche-pflanzen-im-pestizid-test-2026

**6 May 2025: PAN Europe reports that a Greenpeace Poland test found acetamiprid in half of Polish honeys, some above legal limits, and calls for an immediate ban**
- Summary: Acetamiprid was in 25 of 48 honey samples (52%), with about one fifth of those above the MRL. PAN Europe also criticises the EU's later decision to raise the honey MRL.
- Why it matters: indirect, residue evidence that keeps acetamiprid prominent for beekeeping NGOs.
- NGO: PAN Europe (Greenpeace Poland data). https://www.pan-europe.info/blog/neurotoxic-pesticides-polish-honey-exceed-safety-limits

## 5. What NGOs say is coming next

- ANSES decisions on French derogations (acetamiprid for hazelnuts) are due within two months of referral (silence counts as refusal, as Générations Futures describes the Council's reservation). Générations Futures says it will take any derogation granted to court, and POLLINIS says the fight continues.
- EFSA's Article 21 review of acetamiprid (DNT and ED), mandated in May 2025 as the NGOs describe it, is pending. PAN Europe and BeeLife want a second mandate on bees and non-target arthropods under the 2023 Bee Guidance Document. PAN Europe and Générations Futures continue to push for Repr. 1B classification and an EU-wide ban.
- The BPR approval expires on 31 Jan 2027 with no renewal application, as reported by Générations Futures. The group describes household products disappearing from 2027, with a full ban from early 2028. NGOs are likely to watch for any attempt to extend the approval or to bring in a new applicant.
- The Food and Feed Safety Omnibus (proposed entry into force 1 Jan 2027, per the NGOs): PAN Europe, Générations Futures, BeeLife and FoE Europe campaign against unlimited approvals and use acetamiprid as a headline example.
