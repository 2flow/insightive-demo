# NGO activity report: Azamethiphos (CAS 35575-96-3)

## 1. Summary

1 of 1 top signals targets the veterinary-medicine use of azamethiphos (the sea-lice treatment Salmosan in Scottish salmon farming); none target the biocide (PT18) approval directly. In the window, NGOs mention azamethiphos only in passing, as one of the sea-lice chemicals cited in campaigns against open-net salmon farming in Scotland, led by WildFish and the wider Scottish anti-salmon-farming coalition. We found no NGO campaign, letter, legal case or testing on azamethiphos as a fly-control biocide (stickers, baits, sprays in animal housing), and nothing on its EU PT18 evaluation or CLH. The direction is stable and the pressure on the BPR file is low. Any carry-over would come from marine-ecotoxicity arguments, which concern use-specific discharges.

### Tier C: indirect, use-specific

**2025-02-10**
**WildFish publishes a report urging restaurants to drop Scottish farmed salmon, citing among other harms that Mowi doubled its use of the organophosphate pesticide azamethiphos between 2018 and 2023**
`vet medicines` · `UK` · `indirect–use` · `study/report`
- Summary: WildFish's "Reality of Scottish Farmed Salmon" report, based on Scottish Government and company data, contrasts industry marketing with on-farm mortality, sea lice and chemical use. It notes that Mowi "has doubled its use of the organophosphate pesticide azamethiphos" over 2018-2023. The ask is for chefs and restaurants to join the Off the Table campaign and take farmed salmon off menus. There is no regulatory demand on azamethiphos itself.
- Why it matters for the BPR approval: The effect is indirect and weak. It targets veterinary use in aquaculture, and the grounds are use-specific (discharges into sea lochs), not intrinsic hazard. It does keep azamethiphos's toxicity to marine invertebrates in public view, which an environmental-risk debate could pick up, but it makes no demand of the EU biocide regime.
- NGO: WildFish. https://wildfish.org/latest-news/new-wildfish-report-finds-consumers-could-be-misled-as-to-the-reality-of-the-farmed-salmon-they-are-purchasing/
