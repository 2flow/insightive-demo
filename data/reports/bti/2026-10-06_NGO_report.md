# NGO report: Bacillus thuringiensis subsp. israelensis (Bti), PT18

## 1. Summary

1 of 1 top signals targets biocidal use of Bti (BPR); none target the EU approval or the ongoing renewal directly, and none come from another regime. In the window, NGO activity on Bti is thin and local. It is mostly a long-running wetland-conservation critique (France, Germany, Sweden) of large-scale larviciding in protected wetlands, on the grounds that it kills non-target midges (chironomids) and so starves birds, dragonflies and bats. The only verified in-window item is Tour du Valat's May 2025 piece on alternatives to Bti in the Camargue. The pan-EU pesticide NGOs (PAN Europe, PAN Germany, ClientEarth, HEAL, BeeLife and others) published nothing on Bti in the window. Some NGOs (Greenpeace Hungary, Ecologistas en Acción, Xerces) still present Bti as the preferred, less harmful option compared with chemical adulticides. Overall, criticism is aimed at how Bti is used and by whom (protected areas, aerial spraying), not at withdrawing the active substance.

## 2. Top signals

### Tier A: direct

**2025-05-26: Tour du Valat calls for mosquito control in the Camargue to move away from spraying Bti on wetlands and towards protecting villages with traps and other alternatives, citing its own findings of large losses of swallow chicks and dragonflies**
`BPR` · `national:FR` · `direct` · `study/report`

- Summary: The Tour du Valat wetland research foundation has monitored Bti larviciding in the Camargue since 2006. In this piece it restates its findings that about one third of swallow chicks died after their diet changed, that dragonfly abundance and diversity fell by about 50%, and that reed-bed invertebrates declined by 34%. It argues for protecting inhabited areas with CO2 mosquito traps (about 70% less nuisance in a 2015-2017 trial at Le Sambuc), removal of standing water and sterile-male methods, instead of treating thousands of hectares of natural habitat.
- Why it matters for the BPR renewal: It targets the use of Bti biocidal products, so it is direct in regime terms. But it argues against local deployment, not for the EU approval to be refused. Its evidence on non-target effects (chironomids and the knock-on food-web effects) bears on the environmental risk assessment that the Italian evaluating CA is now running, and it could be cited in comments if one is opened. On its own it carries little weight: it is a single national item restating findings from 2015-2020, and there is no campaign or formal request attached.
- NGO and link: Tour du Valat (foundation for Mediterranean wetland conservation): https://tourduvalat.org/presse/alternatives-au-bti-pour-le-controle-des-moustiques-en-camargue/

## 5. What NGOs say is coming next

No NGO has announced a campaign, legal case or consultation response on the EU Bti renewal. Things to watch:
- Seasonal local disputes over Bti in protected wetlands (Camargue, Upper Rhine/KABS, Chiemsee, Nedre Dalälven) recur each spring and summer. Tour du Valat is pushing for alternatives in the Camargue.
- In Germany, the 2025 PT18 self-service ban and its expert-knowledge training scheme (training offer reportedly ending in May 2026) may lead NGOs to comment on how Bti tablets are sold to the public. NABU's comments so far appear only in press reports.
- If the renewal or comparative assessment reaches a public consultation, the chironomid evidence that wetland NGOs and researchers rely on is the most likely basis for NGO comments.
