## 1. Summary

The NGO activity that matters most for this file is PAN Europe's campaign against renewing **phosphine** under the plant protection regulation (Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009). Phosphine is the gas that aluminium phosphide releases, and it is the toxic agent in the BPR entry "aluminium phosphide releasing phosphine". In its position letters to the EU Standing Committee on Plants, Animals, Food and Feed (SCoPAFF), PAN Europe cites EFSA's finding that phosphine's genotoxic potential is a critical area of concern. It argues that mutagenic substances cannot be approved "under any circumstances". The letters went out in December 2025 and March 2026, and again in June 2026 ahead of the 29–30 June vote. None of these letters names aluminium phosphide or biocides. Outside the EU, the South African NGO groundWork (September 2026) asked for aluminium phosphide to be restricted and substituted on acute-poisoning grounds. Overall, NGOs are using hazard arguments against phosphine, but there is no campaign aimed at the BPR PT18 file itself.

## 2. Top signals (ranked by likely impact on the BPR renewal)

### Tier 1: hazard-based, EU, in the focus window

**1. 24 June 2026: PAN Europe asks EU governments to vote down the renewal of phosphine, the gas aluminium phosphide releases, because EFSA flagged it as possibly damaging DNA**
- Summary: PAN Europe's position letter for the 29–30 June 2026 SCoPAFF meeting covers agenda item B.09, the draft Implementing Regulation renewing phosphine. It calls on Member States to "reject the Commission proposal to renew the approval of phosphine". It cites EFSA's peer review, which names genotoxic potential as a critical area of concern based on in vitro, in vivo (somatic cell) and human biomonitoring data. It adds that no threshold mode of action, and so no reference values, could be set. It invokes Annex II point 3.6.2: mutagenic substances "must not be approved under any circumstances".
- Why it matters for the BPR renewal: **Indirect, hazard-based (strong carry-over).** The concern is about the phosphine molecule itself, which is the active agent of aluminium phosphide in PT18. Any finding of genotoxicity or mutagenicity, or a CLH classification for it, applies to the biocide assessment too. The BPR file is already at the Commission-decision stage with a "may be renewed" BPC opinion and CLH opinions recently adopted. This argument could be used to push for a mutagenicity exclusion or substitution discussion, or to challenge the BPR decision once adopted. The PPP renewal is going ahead despite the critical concern, so NGOs have an obvious target for a later challenge. However, PAN Europe has not named aluminium phosphide or the BPR.
- NGO and link: PAN Europe, [SCoPAFF letter, 29–30 June 2026](https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/Letters/2026_06_29-30%20-%20SCoPAFF%20letter.pdf)

### Tier 2: hazard-based, EU, earlier in the window

**2. 5 March 2026 and 8 December 2025: PAN Europe repeatedly urges the EU not to renew phosphine, arguing that evidence it breaks chromosomes rules out renewal**
- Summary: In its 10–11 March 2026 SCoPAFF letter (item A.04, EFSA conclusions), PAN Europe says EFSA's conclusions "preclude the renewal of the substance, particularly in light of clear evidence for its clastogenicity". It calls on Member States "to urgently support the non-renewal of phosphine". The 10–11 December 2025 letter makes the same argument in nearly the same words, calling phosphine "the potentially genotoxic substance phosphine".
- Why it matters for the BPR renewal: **Indirect, hazard-based (strong carry-over).** It shows a sustained position across three committee cycles rather than a one-off, so it is likely to come back as an internal review request or other follow-up once the PPP decision is published. Same caveat: neither letter names aluminium phosphide or biocides.
- NGO and links: PAN Europe, [SCoPAFF letter, 10–11 March 2026](https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/Letters/20260304%20%20SCoPAFF%20letter%2010-11%20March.pdf); [SCoPAFF letter, 10–11 December 2025](https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/Letters/20251204%20SCoPAFF%20letter%2010&11th%20December%20pb.pdf)

### Tier 3: names the substance directly, non-EU, exposure-based

**3. 15 September 2026: South African environmental-justice NGO groundWork asks its government to restrict aluminium phosphide to licensed professionals and favour substitution, citing poisoning risk from uncontrolled phosphine release**
- Summary: groundWork (Friends of the Earth International member) responded to South Africa's proposed notification of aluminium phosphide, dichlorvos and amitraz, which it links to the Rotterdam Convention process. It says there is "a strong public-health and environmental case" for final regulatory action. It highlights the acute hazard of phosphine release and risks to workers, people re-entering fumigated areas, emergency responders and bystanders. It also says the substance is reported to be highly toxic to birds, fish and honeybees. Its "key regulatory argument" is that relying on PPE "is a weak risk-management strategy" and "the hierarchy of controls favours substitution or elimination wherever practicable". It proposes alternatives such as hermetic storage and controlled-atmosphere/CO2 treatments.
- Why it matters for the BPR renewal: **Indirect, mainly use- and exposure-based (weak carry-over).** It does not dispute the hazard data. The case rests on misuse, enforcement gaps and the availability of alternatives in South Africa, which do not transfer directly to EU professional PT18 use. It could matter only if a South African final regulatory action feeds into Rotterdam Convention listing, or if EU NGOs pick up the substitution argument.
- NGO and link: groundWork, [Comments and recommendations on South Africa's proposed notification of Aluminium Phosphide, Dichlorvos and Amitraz (15 Sep 2026)](https://groundwork.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/groundWork-comments-and-recommendations-on-South-Africas-on-the-proposed-notification-of-Aluminium-Phosphide-Dichlorvos-and-Amitraz.pdf)

## 5. What NGOs say is coming next

- PAN Europe treats phosphine as a substance that "cannot be considered to meet the approval criteria". It has repeated this at each SCoPAFF cycle since December 2025. No NGO statement on the outcome of the 29–30 June 2026 vote was found. Neither PAN Europe nor any other NGO has publicly announced an internal review request or litigation on phosphine so far.
- Food and Feed Safety Omnibus (which also amends the BPR): PAN Europe says the file now goes to the European Parliament and then to trilogues, and it is campaigning against longer approvals, grace periods and derogations (press release, 30 Sep 2026).
- South Africa: groundWork's comments feed into South Africa's proposed notification and final regulatory action on aluminium phosphide. groundWork links this to the Rotterdam Convention process and the Global Framework on Chemicals HHP phase-out target (A7). No timeline was given.
