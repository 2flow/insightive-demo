## 1. Summary

4 of 4 top signals target pesticide (PPP) regimes: 3 target the EU renewal of phosphine under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 and 1 targets South Africa's pesticide controls. None target the biocide approval directly. The main signal is PAN Europe's repeated position letters to the EU Standing Committee on Plants, Animals, Food and Feed (SCoPAFF), sent ahead of the December 2025, May 2026 and June 2026 meetings. In them PAN Europe asks Member States to reject the renewal of phosphine because EFSA identified phosphine's genotoxic (clastogenic) potential as a critical area of concern, and it argues that mutagenic substances "must not be approved under any circumstances". The letters do not name aluminium phosphide or biocides. However, phosphine is the active toxic agent in the BPR entry "aluminium phosphide releasing phosphine", so the hazard argument carries over strongly. Outside the EU, groundWork (FoE South Africa) asked in September 2026 for aluminium phosphide to be restricted and substituted, citing its acute poisoning hazard. The direction is hazard-based pressure on phosphine in the PPP regime. There is no NGO campaign on the PT18 file itself.

## 2. Top signals (ranked by likely impact on the BPR renewal)

### 1. 2026-06-24 — PAN Europe asks EU Member States to vote down the renewal of phosphine as a pesticide, citing EFSA's finding that it may damage DNA with no safe threshold
`PPP` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`
- **Summary:** PAN Europe wrote this position letter ahead of the 29–30 June 2026 SCoPAFF meeting, where the draft regulation renewing phosphine was on the agenda (item B.09). It asks Member States to reject the renewal. It cites EFSA's peer-review conclusion that phosphine's genotoxic potential is a critical area of concern, supported by in vitro, in vivo (somatic cells) and human biomonitoring data. It also notes that no threshold mode of action and no toxicological reference values could be set. It invokes Annex II point 3.6.2 of 1107/2009 ("mutagenic substances must not be approved under any circumstances").
- **Why it matters for the BPR renewal:** The effect is indirect but rests on hazard grounds, so it carries over strongly. The genotoxicity concern attaches to phosphine, the substance aluminium phosphide releases, and would apply equally to the PT18 assessment. An NGO-pushed mutagenicity argument also strengthens pressure on the open CLH process. This is the most recent statement and comes from the main EU pesticide NGO, ahead of a vote stage.
- **NGO / link:** PAN Europe — https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/Letters/2026_06_29-30%20-%20SCoPAFF%20letter.pdf

### 2. 2026-04-30 — PAN Europe asks EU Member States to reject the Commission's proposal to renew phosphine, citing clear evidence that it breaks chromosomes
`PPP` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`
- **Summary:** PAN Europe wrote this position letter for the 5–6 May 2026 SCoPAFF meeting, where the phosphine renewal draft was listed as item C.06. It calls on Member States to reject the proposal, citing "clear evidence of phosphine's clastogenicity" and EFSA's critical area of concern. It argues that phosphine cannot meet the renewal criteria of 1107/2009.
- **Why it matters for the BPR renewal:** The effect is indirect and rests on hazard grounds, so it carries over strongly. It shows the argument has been sustained over successive meetings. It falls inside the 6-month focus window.
- **NGO / link:** PAN Europe — https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/Letters/Letter_30042026_SCoPAFF%20letter%205%20May.pdf

### 3. 2025-12-08 — PAN Europe urges EU Member States not to renew phosphine as a pesticide after EFSA flagged it as genotoxic
`PPP` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`
- **Summary:** In its letter for the 10–11 December 2025 SCoPAFF meeting, PAN Europe responds to EFSA's conclusions on "the potentially genotoxic substance phosphine" (item A.04). It states that the conclusions "preclude the renewal of the substance in light of the evidence for its clastogenicity". It asks Member States to "urgently support the non-renewal of phosphine".
- **Why it matters for the BPR renewal:** This is the start of the NGO campaign, and it rests on hazard grounds. It sets out the argument that phosphine meets a PPP cut-off criterion. If adopted in the PPP regime, that argument would likely be raised against the BPR renewal of phosphine-releasing substances.
- **NGO / link:** PAN Europe — https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/Letters/20251204%20SCoPAFF%20letter%2010&11th%20December%20pb.pdf

### 4. 2026-09-15 — South African NGO groundWork asks its government to restrict aluminium phosphide and replace it with safer methods, citing its extreme acute poisoning risk
`PPP` · `national:ZA` · `indirect–use` · `formal request`
- **Summary:** groundWork (a Friends of the Earth International member) responded to South Africa's proposed Rotterdam Convention notification on aluminium phosphide, dichlorvos and amitraz. It says there is "a strong public-health and environmental case" to restrict aluminium phosphide. It argues that relying on PPE and user compliance is a weak strategy given phosphine's acute toxicity, and that "the hierarchy of controls favours substitution or elimination wherever practicable". It lists alternatives: hermetic storage, controlled atmospheres/CO2, IPM and others. It also calls for licensing, gas monitoring and exclusion zones where use continues.
- **Why it matters for the BPR renewal:** The effect is indirect and weak. It concerns South African agricultural and grain-fumigation use and turns on local access, misuse and enforcement, which are use-specific exposure grounds. It does not touch the EU file. However, its substitution argument mirrors the comparative-assessment logic an EU NGO could use, and a South African final regulatory action could later feed into the Rotterdam Convention process.
- **NGO / link:** groundWork — https://groundwork.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/groundWork-comments-and-recommendations-on-South-Africas-on-the-proposed-notification-of-Aluminium-Phosphide-Dichlorvos-and-Amitraz.pdf
