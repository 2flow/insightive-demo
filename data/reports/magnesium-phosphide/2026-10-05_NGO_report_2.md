# NGO activity report: Magnesium phosphide (CAS 12057-74-8), BPR PT18

## 1. Summary

All 3 top signals target the plant protection product (PPP) renewal of phosphine; none target the biocide approval of magnesium phosphide directly, and no NGO names magnesium phosphide. The only consistent NGO activity is from PAN Europe. In its position letters to the EU pesticides committee (SCoPAFF) dated 2025-12-08, 2026-03-05 and 2026-06-24, it asks Member States to non-renew phosphine, the gas magnesium phosphide releases. Its argument rests on EFSA's critical area of concern for genotoxicity (clastogenicity) and on Annex II point 3.6.2 of Regulation 1107/2009 ("mutagenic substances must not be approved"). The argument is about hazard and applies just as well to the phosphine-releasing biocide. PAN Europe's 2026-09-18 letter no longer mentions phosphine, and no NGO source we found says how the PPP vote ended. We found no NGO campaigns, testing, litigation or BPR-specific statements on magnesium phosphide.

## 2. Top signals (ranked by likely impact on the BPR renewal)

Scope note: none of these items names magnesium phosphide. They are included as top signals because the BPR active substance is "magnesium phosphide releasing phosphine", phosphine is the active and toxic moiety, and the brief lists "phosphine fumigant" as a search term. See Method notes.

### 2.1 2026-06-24: PAN Europe asks EU Member States to vote against renewing the grain fumigant phosphine, saying EFSA found it may damage DNA and EU law bans mutagenic pesticides
`PPP` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`

- **Summary:** In its position letter before the 29–30 June 2026 SCoPAFF meeting, PAN Europe asked Member States to reject the Commission's draft regulation renewing phosphine (agenda item B.09). It cites EFSA's peer-review conclusion that phosphine's genotoxic potential is a critical area of concern, based on in vitro, in vivo (somatic cell) and human biomonitoring data. It says no threshold mode of action or reference values could be set, and that Annex II point 3.6.2 bars mutagenic substances from approval "under any circumstances".
- **Why it matters for the BPR renewal:** This is indirect, but it carries over strongly because it rests on hazard. Phosphine is the substance that magnesium phosphide biocides release. A genotoxicity concern with no safe threshold, if formalised (for example through PPP non-renewal or CLH), would weigh directly on the BPR exclusion and risk assessment for magnesium phosphide. This is the most recent and most pointed of the three letters, as it came at the vote stage.
- **NGO:** PAN Europe. [SCoPAFF letter, 29–30 June 2026 (PDF)](https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/Letters/2026_06_29-30%20-%20SCoPAFF%20letter.pdf)

### 2.2 2026-03-05: PAN Europe urges Member States to "urgently" non-renew phosphine, citing clear evidence that it breaks chromosomes
`PPP` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`

- **Summary:** In its letter before the 10–11 March 2026 SCoPAFF meeting, under EFSA conclusions (A.04), PAN Europe says EFSA's conclusions on phosphine "preclude the renewal" in light of "clear evidence for its clastogenicity". It calls on Member States to "urgently support the non-renewal of phosphine". The same letter also attacks the food and feed safety Omnibus, which amends Regulation 528/2012 (see section 3).
- **Why it matters for the BPR renewal:** This is indirect and rests on hazard (genotoxicity of phosphine), so it carries over strongly to the phosphine-releasing biocide. Repeating the demand shows a sustained position rather than a one-off.
- **NGO:** PAN Europe. [SCoPAFF letter, 10–11 March 2026 (PDF)](https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/Letters/20260304%20%20SCoPAFF%20letter%2010-11%20March.pdf)

### 2.3 2025-12-08: PAN Europe first flags phosphine as "potentially genotoxic" and asks Member States to block its renewal after EFSA's peer review
`PPP` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`

- **Summary:** PAN Europe's letter before the 10–11 December 2025 SCoPAFF meeting lists "the potentially genotoxic substance phosphine" among the EFSA conclusions on the agenda. It argues that EFSA's findings (clastogenicity, no threshold, no reference values, critical area of concern) mean phosphine "does not meet the requirements for renewal". It asks Member States to support non-renewal.
- **Why it matters for the BPR renewal:** This is indirect, rests on hazard, and carries over strongly. It is the start of PAN Europe's phosphine position within the window.
- **NGO:** PAN Europe. [SCoPAFF letter, 10–11 December 2025 (PDF)](https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/Letters/20251204%20SCoPAFF%20letter%2010&11th%20December%20pb.pdf)

## 5. What NGOs say is coming next

- PAN Europe's most recent stated demand (2026-06-24) is that Member States reject the Commission's draft regulation renewing phosphine under the PPP Regulation. Its 2026-09-18 letter does not return to phosphine. No NGO source we found reports the outcome, whether that is a vote, a postponement or adoption. Watch the next PAN Europe SCoPAFF letters and any NGO reaction to a phosphine renewal or non-renewal decision.
- If the Commission renews phosphine despite the genotoxicity concern, PAN Europe could seek an internal review under the Aarhus Regulation and then go to court, as it has done for other substances. No NGO has announced this for phosphine; it is our inference, not a stated plan.
- Omnibus: PAN Europe continues to campaign against the food and feed safety Omnibus, which also amends BPR 528/2012. Its letters from June and July 2026 call for a stronger Council position.
