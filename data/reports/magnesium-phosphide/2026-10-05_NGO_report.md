## 1. Summary

No NGO found in the window names magnesium phosphide itself, and none targets the BPR (PT18) file. The relevant NGO activity is about **phosphine**, the gas that magnesium phosphide releases and that does the toxic work. PAN Europe used its letters to the EU pesticides committee (SCoPAFF) of 8 December 2025 and 24 June 2026 to call for **non-renewal of phosphine under the plant protection regulation (Reg. 1107/2009)**. Its argument is that EFSA flagged phosphine's genotoxic (clastogenic) potential as a critical area of concern, and that mutagens "must not be approved under any circumstances". This is a hazard-based argument about the active moiety, so it would carry over to magnesium phosphide's biocide file if it gains traction. So far it is a single NGO making the point in committee letters. There has been no press campaign, litigation or testing. More broadly, PAN Europe and Générations Futures oppose the Food and Feed Safety Omnibus, which also amends the BPR (528/2012). That opposition is class-level and does not name the substance. Direction: low volume but hazard-led and rising, and it coincides with the phosphine PPP renewal decision.

## 2. Top signals (ranked by likely impact on the BPR renewal)

**Tier 1: hazard-based, about the released active moiety (phosphine)**

1. **Date:** 24 June 2026 (letter for the 29–30 June 2026 SCoPAFF meeting)
   - **Title:** PAN Europe asks EU Member States to vote down the renewal of phosphine, the toxic gas released by magnesium phosphide fumigants, citing EU food-safety experts' finding that it may damage DNA
   - **Summary:** PAN Europe's position letter to SCoPAFF covers agenda item B.09, the draft Implementing Regulation renewing phosphine's approval. It says EFSA's peer review recognises phosphine's genotoxic potential as a critical area of concern, based on in vitro studies, in vivo studies in somatic cells and human biomonitoring data. It adds that no threshold mode of action and no reference values could be set, and it cites Annex II point 3.6.2 (mutagens must not be approved). The letter says: "We call on you to reject the Commission's proposal for approval of phosphine."
   - **Why it matters for BPR:** Indirect, because the target is the PPP regime and the letter does not name magnesium phosphide or biocides. However, the argument rests on **hazard grounds** (genotoxicity of phosphine, the agent every metal-phosphide product releases), which carry over strongly. If the mutagenicity argument is reflected in harmonised classification, it would bear on the BPR exclusion criteria. It is the most recent signal and falls within the 6-month focus window, while the BPR renewal is at the Commission-decision stage.
   - **NGO / link:** PAN Europe – https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/Letters/2026_06_29-30%20-%20SCoPAFF%20letter.pdf

2. **Date:** 8 December 2025 (letter for the 10–11 December 2025 SCoPAFF meeting)
   - **Title:** PAN Europe urges EU countries to block renewal of phosphine fumigant on genotoxicity grounds after EFSA's review
   - **Summary:** Agenda item A.04 covered EFSA conclusions on "the potentially genotoxic substance phosphine". In its letter on that item, PAN Europe says the EFSA conclusions "preclude the renewal of the substance in light of the evidence for its clastogenicity" and asks Member States to "urgently support the non-renewal of phosphine".
   - **Why it matters for BPR:** Same as above. It is indirect (PPP regime, magnesium phosphide not named) but hazard-based on the released moiety. It shows that PAN Europe has kept the same position across two committee cycles.
   - **NGO / link:** PAN Europe – https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/Letters/20251204%20SCoPAFF%20letter%2010&11th%20December%20pb.pdf
