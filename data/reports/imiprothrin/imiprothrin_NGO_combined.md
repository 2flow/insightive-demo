**PAN International keeps imiprothrin on its global list of Highly Hazardous Pesticides to be phased out, citing its high toxicity to bees**
`cross-regime` · `international` · `indirect–hazard` · `study/report`
- Summary: PAN International's list of Highly Hazardous Pesticides (HHP) is updated every year, most recently in December 2024. It continues to include imiprothrin. PAN North America's PesticideInfo database marks imiprothrin as a PAN International HHP, flagged as a bee toxicant ("considered highly toxic to bees"). The database also notes that its reproductive/developmental and endocrine effects are "insufficiently studied". PAN uses the list to call for a global phase-out of HHPs, but there is no EU campaign specific to imiprothrin.
- Why it matters for the BPR renewal: This acts indirectly and on hazard grounds, so it would carry over to any regime. PAN-network NGOs could cite it if the 2029 renewal or the CLH outcome draws attention. Its weight is low because the listing is long-standing, it is not aimed at the EU biocide file, and bee exposure from indoor crawling-insect products is limited. If the CLH outcome confirms Repr. 2 (a hazard flag already open in PACT), it could give NGOs a second hazard hook. No NGO has picked this up so far.
