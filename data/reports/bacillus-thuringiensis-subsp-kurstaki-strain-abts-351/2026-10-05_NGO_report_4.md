## 1. Summary

None of the 2 top signals targets the BPR approval of Btk directly. One is tagged BPR but concerns local use, and none targets a PPP, REACH/CLP, water or residue decision. The BPR item is a local German criticism of how the biocide is used against oak processionary moth. The other is a Canadian citizens' campaign against aerial forestry spraying.

The German item is from NABU Kreisverband Minden-Lübbecke (2026-06-13). It says Bt is "harmlessly often called a biocide" but is in fact an insecticide that also kills other caterpillars and flies. It asks for spraying to be restricted and better targeted, not banned.

The Canadian Kelowna Citizens Safety Association (KCSA) says British Columbia had no aerial Btk spraying in 2025 and has only a reduced 2026 programme. KCSA says it will keep monitoring that programme.

No major EU NGO (PAN Europe, PAN Germany, ClientEarth, HEAL, Générations Futures, GLOBAL 2000, BeeLife, Buglife, Wildlife Trusts, etc.) campaigns against Btk. Where they mention Bt at all, they present it as a low-impact alternative. Overall direction: stable, with low pressure on the BPR renewal (CA France, expiry 2027-02-28). The criticism found is about how the product is used and its effects on non-target species, not about its intrinsic hazard.

- **Date:** 2026-06-13
  **Title:** German nature NGO asks its district to spray Bacillus thuringiensis against oak processionary moth only where people are proven to be at risk, citing the killing of other butterfly and moth caterpillars and fly species
  `BPR` · `national:DE` · `indirect–use` · `statement`
  **Summary:** NABU Kreisverband Minden-Lübbecke questions the district's control of oak processionary moth. It says Bacillus thuringiensis, "verharmlosend oft als Biozid bezeichnet", is an insecticide that also kills non-target caterpillars and flies. NABU asks for chemical control only where a real danger is documented, for infestations to be prioritised by location, and for any treatment to be timed carefully and applied only in suitable weather.
  **Why it matters:** This concerns the PT18 use itself (oak processionary moth treatment), so it is the closest NGO criticism to the BPR file. It acts only indirectly because it targets local use decisions, not the active-substance approval. It rests on use-specific, non-target-ecology grounds, which carry over weakly. At most it could feed into product-authorisation conditions (timing, risk mitigation) rather than non-renewal. The page says "Bacillus thuringiensis" without naming the subspecies or strain. It is treated as Btk because of the context.
  **NGO:** NABU Kreisverband Minden-Lübbecke: https://www.nabu-minden-luebbecke.de/aktuelles/nabu-hinterfragt-bekaempfung-des-eichenprozessionsspinners-in-minden-luebbecke/

- **Date:** 2026 (undated page update reporting the 2026 programme; no exact day shown)
  **Title:** Canadian citizens' group says it has helped cut aerial Btk spraying in British Columbia and will monitor the 2026 spray, citing public-health risks and lack of informed consent
  `cross-regime` · `international` · `indirect–use` · `campaign`
  **Summary:** KCSA says aerial spraying was halted in BC in 2025. It says the spring 2026 programme is limited to Delta/Tsawwassen (38 ha) and Squamish (121 ha).   **Why it matters:** Very indirect. This is Canadian aerial forestry spraying and has no EU regulatory hook. Its arguments about bystander exposure are use-specific and carry over weakly. It is relevant only as a sign that "Btk is not harmless" arguments exist that EU groups could pick up. Tagged `cross-regime` because the case falls outside the EU regime categories (forestry pest control under Canadian law).
