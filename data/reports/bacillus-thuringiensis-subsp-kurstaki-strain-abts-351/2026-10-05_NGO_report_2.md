## 1. Summary

NGO activity on Btk in the window is thin, local and use-specific. The only in-window EU item found is a NABU district group in Germany (2026-06-13). It criticises the municipal spraying of Bacillus thuringiensis against oak processionary moth because the spray also kills non-target caterpillars and harms birds that feed on them. It asks for spraying to be restricted, not banned. A Canadian citizens' group (KCSA, British Columbia) has campaigned against aerial Btk spraying (Foray 48B) on human-exposure grounds. It claims credit for no BC spray in 2025 and a much smaller 2026 programme. No major EU NGO (PAN Europe, PAN Germany, ClientEarth, HEAL, Générations Futures, GLOBAL 2000, BeeLife, etc.) has campaigned against Btk, and where they mention Bt they treat it as a preferred low-risk alternative. Overall direction: stable, with low pressure on the BPR renewal. The criticism concerns how and where the biocide is used (non-target Lepidoptera, birds), not its intrinsic hazard.

## 2. Top signals

**Tier 1: indirect, use-specific (EU)**

- **Date:** 2026-06-13
  **Title:** German nature NGO asks the local district to spray Bacillus thuringiensis against oak processionary moth only where people are proven to be at risk, citing the killing of non-target butterfly and moth caterpillars and harm to birds
  **Summary:** NABU Kreisverband Minden-Lübbecke questioned the district's control of oak processionary moth. It says that although Bacillus thuringiensis is "verharmlosend oft als Biozid bezeichnet", it is an insecticide that also kills other butterfly caterpillars and fly species. NABU asks for chemical control only where there is documented danger to people, treatment only on the first two larval stages (by mid-May), spraying only in calm and dry weather, and proper planning and documentation.
  **Why it matters:** Indirect. This is the BPR PT18 use itself (oak processionary moth biocide treatments), so it is the most relevant kind of NGO criticism for this file. But it is a local group, it rests on use-specific exposure and non-target-ecology grounds, and it asks for restricted and better-targeted use, not non-renewal. It could feed into product-authorisation conditions (timing, risk-mitigation measures) more than into the active-substance renewal. The page names "Bacillus thuringiensis" and does not state the subspecies or strain. The judgement that it concerns Btk is based on context (oak processionary moth biocide use in Germany).
  **NGO:** NABU Kreisverband Minden-Lübbecke: https://www.nabu-minden-luebbecke.de/aktuelles/nabu-hinterfragt-bekaempfung-des-eichenprozessionsspinners-in-minden-luebbecke/

**Tier 2: non-EU, human-exposure framing**

- **Date:** 2026-01 (KCSA's account of the provincial announcement; no exact day given)
  **Title:** Canadian citizens' group claims it has cut aerial Btk spraying in British Columbia and pledges to keep watching the 2026 spray, citing the risk to children, families and pets from pesticide exposure
  **Summary:** The Kelowna Citizens Safety Association says aerial Btk spraying was halted in BC in 2025 after its campaign. It calls the 2026 programme, limited to Delta (38 ha) and Squamish (121 ha), "a major reduction" and "a significant victory for community advocacy". KCSA commits to "closely monitor the 2026 aerial BTK spray program" and to inform communities about timing, substances, risks and alternatives. Its archived 2024 campaign also raised Health Canada incident reports and undisclosed co-formulants in Foray 48B, and it filed an appeal to the Ministry of Forests (2024-04-17, outside the window).
  **Why it matters:** Very indirect. The group is Canadian and the case is about aerial forestry spraying under Canadian law, not the EU BPR. Its concerns about bystander exposure and co-formulants are use- and product-specific and carry over weakly. It is useful mainly as a sign that "Btk is not harmless" arguments are circulating and could be picked up by EU groups.
  **NGO:** Kelowna Citizens Safety Association: https://kelownacsa.org/aerial-spraying-local-and-provincal (archive: https://kelownacsa.org/btk-spraying-program-2024-and-2025)
