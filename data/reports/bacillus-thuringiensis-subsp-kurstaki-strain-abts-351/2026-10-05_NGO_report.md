## 1. Summary

NGO activity on Btk in the window is thin, local and focused on how it is used, not on hazard. The only dated in-window item that names Bacillus thuringiensis is a June 2026 press release from a NABU district group. It criticises roadside spraying against oak processionary moth (OPM) as badly timed and harmful to non-target butterfly caterpillars. The Dutch Vlinderstichting keeps up its general opposition to "biological or chemical" OPM control, updated January 2026, but does not name Bt. Older German and UK material (BUND, Butterfly Conservation, 2014–2021) makes the same non-target Lepidoptera argument. No large EU NGO (PAN Europe, ClientEarth, HEAL, Générations Futures, GLOBAL 2000 etc.) is campaigning against Btk. Some, such as Générations Futures and BeeLife, describe Bt in a neutral or positive way, as a low-impact alternative. Overall direction: steady, low-level concern about non-target effects in OPM programmes. Nothing points at the BPR renewal.

## 2. Top signals (ranked by likely impact on BPR renewal)

**Tier 1: in window, names the substance**

1. **13 June 2026: NABU Minden-Lübbecke questions Bt spraying against oak processionary moth**
   - Summary: Press release from the NABU district group about roadside spraying near Rahden-Tonnenheide. The district road authority confirmed to NABU that a *Bacillus thuringiensis* suspension was used. NABU says the spraying came too late (after the early larval stages), in wind with rain forecast, so it was ineffective and wasted public money. It also says Bt "is often played down as a biocide" but is an insecticide that also kills other butterfly caterpillars and fly species, noting that over 200 butterfly species live on oaks. NABU demands spraying only where there is a documented hazard, only in the first two larval stages, and only in calm, dry weather.
   - Why it matters: Indirect and weak. The argument is about how the product is used (timing, drift, targeting). It does not challenge the substance's hazard profile, and it accepts that Bt does not affect bees, lacewings or ladybirds. It ties into the non-target arthropod and Lepidoptera part of the PT18 environmental risk assessment, and into Union-authorisation conditions such as timing and drift mitigation. It does not challenge approval. Scope note: the release says only "Bacillus thuringiensis". It is included because the PT18 OPM products in Germany are Btk-based (Foray ES / Dipel ES), but the strain is not confirmed by NABU.
   - NGO: NABU Kreisverband Minden-Lübbecke, https://www.nabu-minden-luebbecke.de/aktuelles/nabu-hinterfragt-bekaempfung-des-eichenprozessionsspinners-in-minden-luebbecke/

**Tier 2: in window, class-level (Bt not named)**

2. **January 2026 (page update): De Vlinderstichting oak processionary caterpillar management page**
   - Summary: The Dutch Butterfly Conservation foundation's OPM page refers to a Gelderland province map (early January 2026) showing where spraying is not allowed. It urges municipalities to avoid "chemical or biological" control as far as possible, because these methods kill non-target caterpillars and affect the food chain.
   - Why it matters: Indirect and weak. It concerns exposure and use, and covers biological control as a class without naming Btk. In the Netherlands Bt aizawai is also used, and that variant is out of scope. It may support local restrictions on use rather than any change at EU level.
   - NGO: De Vlinderstichting, https://vlinderstichting.nl/eikenprocessierups
