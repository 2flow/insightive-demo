## 1. Summary

There are no top signals: none of the NGO material found targets the biocide approval of Eucalyptus citriodora oil, hydrated, cyclized, and none targets it in any other regime. NGOs mention it only in consumer advice and product tests for mosquito repellents. Here it usually appears as "PMD" or "Citriodiol" and is described as the one plant-based repellent with good evidence behind it. UK consumer group Which? recommended it in July and August 2026. German consumer and environmental groups add standard cautions: it irritates the eyes, can cause skin or allergic reactions, protects for less time than DEET or icaridin, and, as one undated page notes, may harm aquatic organisms. No NGO campaign, legal action, formal request or testing aimed at restricting the substance was found. If anything, the overall tone favours it as a "natural" alternative to DEET.

- Pages opened with WebFetch to confirm content: Which? (2), PAN Germany, VZHH (2), Umweltinstitut, Test-Achats. The EWG page could not be accessed (403).
- Scope decisions: NGOs refer to the substance as "PMD", "Citriodiol" or "oil of lemon eucalyptus". These names were treated as this substance, because the PMD-rich refined oil is the active substance. Synthetic PMD (CAS 42822-86-6) is technically a separate substance, and the consumer pages do not always say which one they mean. The variant listed in the brief (Eucalyptus citriodora oil and citronellal, hydrated, cyclized) is covered by the same terms. Plain or unrefined eucalyptus or citronella oils were excluded. Consumer-organisation advice pages were treated as NGO material but kept at lower relevance, because they do not target any regulatory decision. Press coverage (SRF Kassensturz, Deutsche Apotheker Zeitung, BNNVARA Kassa) and manufacturer sites (Citrefine) were excluded as sources.
- Overall judgement: there is no NGO pressure on the BPR file. The substance is obscure in NGO terms and is generally presented as a preferred "natural" repellent.
