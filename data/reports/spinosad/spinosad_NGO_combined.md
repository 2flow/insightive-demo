**2025-04-07: PAN Netherlands puts spinosad on a "black list" of 10 insecticides it says are too dangerous for home and garden use, and asks retailers to stop selling them by 2027, citing bee toxicity and possible reproductive and thyroid effects**
`cross-regime` · `national:NL` · `direct` · `campaign`
- Summary: PAN-NL surveyed home-and-garden pest products sold online and at Dutch garden centres (Welkoop, Praxis, Intratuin, Hornbach and others). It says its survey covers products authorised as plant protection products, as biocides and as veterinary medicines. It named two spinosad ant products, "Pokon Tegen Mieren Korrels" and the "ECOstyle Mierenlokdoos", an ant bait box for use "zowel binnen als buiten het huis" (both inside and outside the house). It describes spinosad as "zeer giftig voor bijen" (very toxic to bees), says it may affect reproduction and may be toxic to the thyroid, spleen, liver and lymph nodes, and puts it on a "ZWARTE LIJST" (black list). It asks retailers and webshops to drop the listed products and to sell no such products for non-professional use by 2027, and asks government to ban chemical pesticides for non-professional use. The report was released alongside a broadcast on AVROTROS "Radar" (NPO2).
- Why it matters for the BPR renewal: this acts directly. An ant bait for indoor use is a PT18 biocidal product, and the Netherlands is the evaluating CA for the spinosad BPR renewal. The argument rests mainly on hazard (bee toxicity, suspected reproductive and organ toxicity), which carries over strongly. The demand itself is use-specific: it targets non-professional and consumer uses and retail listings, not the active-substance approval. In practice this could feed Ctgb product-authorisation decisions or retailer de-listing more than the EU active-substance decision.
- NGO: and link: PAN Netherlands, [report](https://www.pan-netherlands.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/onderzoeksrapport.pdf)
- Also: [press release](https://www.pan-netherlands.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/persbericht.pdf)

**2026-04-23: PAN Netherlands finds spinosad residues on garden-centre plants, calls it a "highly hazardous pesticide" and asks the government to phase out all such pesticides, citing harm to insects**
`PPP` · `national:NL` · `indirect–hazard` · `study/report`
- Summary: This is PAN-NL's sixth annual test of plants that attract insects, bought on 2026-04-01 from Welkoop, Praxis and Intratuin. Spinosad (spinosyn A and D) was found on 1 of 17 conventional plants (a Dianthus from Intratuin, 0.057 mg/kg) and on an organic mint plant (0.219 mg/kg). The report lists spinosad among 13 detected substances classed as Highly Hazardous Pesticides on the PAN International HHP list (2024 edition). Its demands to government include "Uitfasering van ... bestrijdingsmiddelen die als zeer gevaarlijk (HHP) ... zijn geclassificeerd" (phase-out of pesticides classified as highly hazardous, HHP), zero tolerance for insect-toxic insecticide residues on plants, and EU maximum residue limits for ornamentals.
- Why it matters for the BPR renewal: indirect. It targets ornamental-plant PPP use, but the demand to phase out spinosad as an HHP rests on hazard grounds (bee toxicity), which carry over to the biocide file. Spinosad is a minor finding in the report: it is not one of the main insecticides discussed. The organic-mint finding is framed neutrally as a residue of an approved organic-use insecticide.
- NGO: and link: PAN Netherlands, [Pesticiden in Tuinplanten 2026](https://www.pan-netherlands.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Rapport-Tuinplanten-onderzoek-23-april-2026-gecomprimeerd.pdf)

**2026-05-20: GLOBAL 2000 and the Upper Austria Chamber of Labour find "highly bee-toxic" pesticides, spinosad among them, on "bee-friendly" garden plants and ask for a ban on insect-hazardous pesticides on such plants, citing harm to pollinators**
`PPP` · `national:AT` · `indirect–use` · `study/report`
- Summary: GLOBAL 2000 tested 30 plants from Austrian garden centres, DIY stores, furniture stores, supermarkets and nurseries for more than 600 pesticides. It names spinosad as one of nine highly bee-toxic active substances found (with acetamiprid, captan, chlorpyrifos, deltamethrin, flupyradifurone, lambda-cyhalothrin, milbemectin and pirimicarb). Its demands are: a ban on highly insect-hazardous pesticides on "bee-friendly" plants, regular official residue controls, an import ban on plants carrying unapproved pesticides, stricter EU plant-passport criteria, support for organic ornamentals, and no synthetic pesticides in home and community gardens. The publication page does not say how many samples contained spinosad.
- Why it matters for the BPR renewal: weak and indirect. The demand is limited to ornamental-plant PPP use. Bee toxicity is a hazard property, but the remedy is use-specific, so it carries over to the PT18 file only weakly.
