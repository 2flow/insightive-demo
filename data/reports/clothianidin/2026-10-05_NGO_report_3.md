## 1. Summary

None of the 7 top signals targets the biocide approval directly. 3 target food residues, 3 target PPP (EU exports and US registration), and 1 is a cross-regime demand. Since 2024-10 NGOs have named clothianidin mainly as an example of an EU-banned neonicotinoid that is still exported by EU companies and comes back as residues in imported food: foodwatch's May 2026 tests, PAN Europe's February 2025 report and Greenpeace/Unearthed's September 2025 export investigation all make this point. The EU-level policy fight is now about the Food and Feed Safety Omnibus (December 2025 proposal), which PAN Europe and foodwatch want rejected. In the UK, Buglife's January 2025 parliamentary briefing argues that substances banned in agriculture should be banned in all other sectors, including domestic products. This is the argument closest to the PT18 file, but it is not aimed at the EU BPR. In the US, Beyond Pesticides (comments to EPA) and the Center for Food Safety (FOIA suit) continue to press hazard and treated-seed concerns. Overall the pressure is steady but indirect. No NGO was found campaigning on the clothianidin PT18 renewal or on clothianidin biocidal products.

### Tier B: indirect, hazard-based

#### 1. 2025-01-16 — Buglife asks UK Parliament to ban pesticides like clothianidin, already banned in farming, in every other use including domestic products, because of their toxicity to bees and other insects
`cross-regime` · `UK` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`
- **Summary:** In its parliamentary briefing "The use and impact of neonicotinoids and other pesticides beyond agriculture", Buglife says neonicotinoids banned for farm use in 2018 are still used in vet flea treatments, forestry, aquaculture and domestic products. It names clothianidin as a toxic breakdown product of thiamethoxam, and as one of the neonicotinoids found in 70% of potted plants in a garden-centre survey. One of its demands is that "Pesticides banned for use in agriculture must be banned for use in all other sectors including veterinary flea treatments, forestry, aquaculture and domestic products."
- **Why it matters for BPR:** This is the only item found that argues against non-agricultural uses (the space where PT18 sits) on the grounds of the substance's agricultural ban. It rests on hazard grounds (bee and invertebrate toxicity, persistence, leaching to water), which carry over strongly between regimes. It is UK-focused and works at class level, so its effect on the EU renewal is indirect. Still, it is the argument most easily reused against a PT18 renewal of a candidate for substitution.
- **NGO / link:** Buglife — https://cdn.buglife.org.uk/downloads/Buglife-Briefing-Neonicotinoids-beyond-agriculture-January-2025-Website-Copy.pdf

#### 6. 2024-10-24 — Beyond Pesticides asks the US EPA to cancel clothianidin, imidacloprid and thiamethoxam, citing developmental neurotoxicity, endocrine disruption and risks to workers, bees and aquatic life
`PPP` · `international` · `indirect–hazard` · `formal request`
- **Summary:** In formal comments on EPA's draft risk assessments (including docket EPA-HQ-OPP-2011-0865, clothianidin), Beyond Pesticides cites a 2024 review of unpublished rodent DNT studies that found brain-tissue shrinkage for five neonicotinoids including clothianidin. It also points to evidence of endocrine disruption and to EPA's own finding of high seed-treatment worker exposure. It notes that neonicotinoids are also used on pets (flea treatments) and in residential and commercial indoor uses, and urges EPA to "immediately remove all uses of clothianidin, imidacloprid, and thiamethoxam".
- **Why it matters for BPR:** The arguments are hazard-based (DNT, ED, aquatic toxicity), which carry over strongly. The DNT and ED points could resurface in an EU human-health or ED assessment. The target is US FIFRA, so the effect on the EU file is indirect.
- **NGO / link:** Beyond Pesticides — https://www.beyondpesticides.org/assets/media/documents/BP%20Comments%20on%20Neonicotinoids%20October%202024.pdf

### Tier C: indirect, use-specific

#### 2. 2026-05-19 — foodwatch's own tests find clothianidin among the most common banned pesticides in tea, rice and spices sold in four EU countries, and foodwatch demands zero residue limits for banned pesticides and an end to their export
`food residues` · `EU` · `indirect–use` · `study/report`
- **Summary:** foodwatch had 64 tea, rice and spice products from AT, DE, FR and NL tested (bought 13–31 March 2026). Clothianidin was among the most frequently detected non-approved pesticides (10–20 detections, alongside bifenthrin, thiamethoxam and spirotetramat). The report says, citing ECHA export notifications for 2024–2025, that clothianidin was exported from 8 EU Member States to 35 third countries. It calls for a ban on exporting non-approved pesticides, automatic lowering of their MRLs to the limit of detection, and rejection of the Food and Feed Safety Omnibus.
- **Why it matters for BPR:** It keeps clothianidin publicly labelled as a "banned, harmful" substance in the EU in the past six months. The concern is mostly about use (residues from farm use outside the EU), so it carries over weakly to a PT18 biocide approval. Its attack on the Omnibus, which would make unlimited approvals the rule, is horizontal.
- **NGO / link:** foodwatch International — https://www.foodwatch.org/fileadmin/-INT/pesticides/banned_pesticides_on_the_menu_residues_found_in_tea_rice_and_spices.pdf (French press release, which does not name clothianidin: https://www.foodwatch.org/fr/actualites/pesticides-interdits-europe-foodwatch-confirme-presence-substances-dangereuses-dans-nos-assiettes)

#### 7. 2026-05-05 — Center for Food Safety sues the US EPA for records on how seed coated with clothianidin and other neonicotinoids is disposed of, citing harm to health and ecosystems from treated-seed waste
`PPP` · `international` · `indirect–use` · `litigation`
- **Summary:** CFS filed a FOIA suit to force EPA to release documents, first requested in 2023, about seed treated with acetamiprid, clothianidin, imidacloprid and thiamethoxam being sent to ethanol plants. CFS says unregulated disposal of coated seed has caused severe human-health and ecological harm. It is also pursuing a separate Ninth Circuit appeal against EPA's "treated article exemption" for coated seeds.
- **Why it matters for BPR:** This is an access-to-documents action about US treated-seed use and waste. It is use-specific and outside the EU, so it carries over weakly. It mainly shows that litigation pressure on clothianidin continues in the US.
- **NGO / link:** Center for Food Safety — https://www.centerforfoodsafety.org/press-releases/7125/center-for-food-safety-sues-epa-to-force-release-of-pesticide-treated-seed-documents

#### 3. 2025-09-23 — Greenpeace's investigative unit shows EU companies still export thousands of tonnes of clothianidin and thiamethoxam products banned in the EU, and campaigners demand an EU export ban
`PPP` · `EU` · `indirect–use` · `study/report`
- **Summary:** Unearthed (Greenpeace UK), working with Public Eye, analysed 2024 EU export notifications. It found about 4,600 tonnes of products containing thiamethoxam and clothianidin notified for export, mainly to Ukraine, Russia and Brazil, and the overall banned-pesticide export trade growing to about 122,000 tonnes. It reports calls for EU-wide legislation to ban these exports.
- **Why it matters for BPR:** It reinforces the message that clothianidin is too harmful for EU fields. The regime is PPP/export (PIC). It does not touch biocide uses, and the effect on the BPR file is weak and reputational.
- **NGO / link:** Greenpeace UK (Unearthed) — https://unearthed.greenpeace.org/2025/09/23/eu-banned-pesticide-trade-expands-despite-promises/

#### 4. 2025-04 — PAN UK warns that Great Britain loosened residue limits for clothianidin, which it lists as a highly hazardous pesticide, to Codex levels above EU limits, and asks for alignment with EU standards
`food residues` · `UK` · `indirect–use` · `study/report`
- **Summary:** PAN UK's briefing "Divergence of GB and EU pesticide standards" shows that HSE's January 2025 adoption of Codex MRLs raised GB clothianidin limits for tomatoes, peppers, aubergines, okra, cumin and other produce above EU values. It flags clothianidin as one of 3 HHPs in that tranche, and notes the EU limit for tomatoes falls to 0.01 mg/kg from March 2026. It recommends that any UK–EU SPS agreement restore and keep dynamic alignment with EU pesticide standards.
- **Why it matters for BPR:** The HHP label rests on hazard. The ask, however, is about residues in food in the UK, so it carries over weakly to the EU PT18 renewal.
- **NGO / link:** PAN UK — https://www.pan-uk.org/site/wp-content/uploads/PANUK_Divergence_GB_EU_pesticides_14Apr2025.pdf

#### 5. 2025-02-10 — PAN Europe finds bee-killing neonicotinoids including clothianidin still turning up in imported food, and demands a ban on residues of EU-banned pesticides and on their export
`food residues` · `EU` · `indirect–use` · `study/report`
- **Summary:** PAN Europe's piece "The return of EU-banned pesticides: dangerous residues found in imported food" draws on its analysis of more than 48,000 samples. It names clothianidin among the "bee-killing neurotoxic insecticides" still found in plant-based foods sold in Europe. It asks the EU to ban imports of food carrying residues of banned substances and to end exports of hazardous pesticides.
- **Why it matters for BPR:** It is the same residue/export framing as items 2 and 3, from the NGO most active on EU pesticide law. The concern mentions hazard (neurotoxic, bee-toxic), but the ask is limited to food and trade, so it carries over weakly.
- **NGO / link:** PAN Europe — https://www.pan-europe.info/node/3975

## 5. What NGOs say is coming next

- **Food and Feed Safety Omnibus:** PAN Europe and foodwatch are lobbying the European Parliament and Council to reject it (unlimited approvals, weaker MRL reviews, a limited and non-automatic fix for residues of banned pesticides). Negotiations continue through 2026, and PAN Europe says 49 pesticides could get unlimited approval from 2027-01-01.
- **Export ban:** the End Toxic Pesticide Trade Coalition (600+ organisations) and foodwatch continue to press the Commission to deliver its 2020 promise to ban exports of EU-banned pesticides, which would cover clothianidin PPP exports.
- **Derogations:** PAN Europe wants further infringement procedures after the Romania case.
- **US:** CFS's FOIA suit is pending, and CFS/PANNA's Ninth Circuit appeal on the treated-seed exemption is pending (argued September 2026 per press reports). EPA is to decide on NRDC's tolerance-revocation petition by 2027-04-30 (unverified on the NGO's own page).
- **UK:** Buglife is pressing for pesticides banned in agriculture to be banned in all other sectors, and for publication of the UK National Action Plan.
