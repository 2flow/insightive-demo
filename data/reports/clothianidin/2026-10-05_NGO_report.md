## 1. Summary

No NGO has campaigned on clothianidin as a biocide (PT18) or on its BPR renewal in the window. No NGO has commented on its BPR approval expiry or the postponement of that expiry. When NGOs name clothianidin, they treat it as an already-banned farm neonicotinoid in three ways:
- **Food residues:** it is still found in imported food (foodwatch lab tests, 2026-05; a joint statement by 18 NGOs and trade unions, 2025-06).
- **Rivers:** it pollutes English rivers (Wildlife and Countryside Link and partners, 2024-12).
- **UK ban:** UK charities welcome government plans to make the farm ban permanent in law (The Wildlife Trusts, 2024-12).

The only demand that reaches non-farm uses comes from Buglife (2025-01): neonicotinoids banned in agriculture should be banned "in all other sectors". It is UK-focused and aimed at flea treatments, forestry and domestic products, not EU biocides.

In the past six months, PAN Europe and BeeLife (2026-05-20) cited clothianidin only as a legal precedent. Their pressure is now on acetamiprid, flupyradifurone and the Omnibus proposal for unlimited approvals.

Overall direction: clothianidin's hazard is treated as settled. NGOs have moved on to "toxic boomerang" trade issues and other neonicotinoids. Their pressure on the PT18 file is indirect and low.

## 2. Top signals (ranked by likely impact on the BPR renewal)

### Tier 1: closest to the biocide file

**2025-01 (parliamentary briefing; PDF metadata 2025-01-16)**
- **Title:** Buglife asks for neonicotinoids banned in farming to be banned in every other sector, including domestic products, citing harm to bees, wild invertebrates and freshwater life.
- **Summary:** The briefing is titled "The use and impact of neonicotinoids and other pesticides beyond agriculture". It argues that the focus on farm use "is obscuring the opportunity to address these highly toxic chemicals in use in other sectors". On clothianidin specifically:
  - "thiamethoxam decaying into clothianidin" is also toxic to insect life;
  - clothianidin was found in garden-centre plants.
  Among its demands: "Pesticides banned for use in agriculture must be banned for use in all other sectors including veterinary flea treatments, forestry, aquaculture and domestic products."
- **Why it matters:** This is the only NGO argument found that targets non-agricultural uses of the farm-banned neonicotinoids, which is the situation clothianidin is in as an approved PT18 biocide. The effect is indirect. It is aimed at the UK Parliament, not the EU BPR, and biocides are not named. It rests on hazard grounds (toxicity to bees and invertebrates, persistence in water), which carry over strongly between regimes. If EU NGOs pick up the "banned in farming, banned everywhere" framing, a clothianidin PT18 renewal would be an obvious target.
- **NGO and link:** Buglife: https://cdn.buglife.org.uk/downloads/Buglife-Briefing-Neonicotinoids-beyond-agriculture-January-2025-Website-Copy.pdf

### Tier 2: hazard and fate evidence that carries over

**2026-05-19 (lab test)**
- **Title:** foodwatch asks the EU to stop banned pesticides, including clothianidin, coming back in imported food, citing residues found in rice, tea and spices.
- **Summary:** foodwatch tested 64 products (rice, tea, paprika, cumin, curry) bought in the Netherlands, France, Austria and Germany. It lists "the bee-killing pesticides Clothianidin, Thiametoxam, and Imadacloprid" among the frequently detected non-approved pesticides. Its demands are:
  - an EU export ban on unapproved pesticides;
  - residue limits for banned substances set at the lowest possible level;
  - rejection of the Food and Feed Omnibus.
- **Why it matters:** This is the most recent NGO item naming clothianidin, and it falls in the six-month focus window. Its effect on the BPR file is indirect: it is about food residues and trade. It keeps clothianidin's "bee-killing, banned" label in public view, which is hazard-based framing. The specific exposure route (imported food) carries over weakly to PT18 uses.
- **NGO and link:** foodwatch: https://www.foodwatch.org/en/lab-tests-foodwatch-finds-eu-banned-pesticides-in-rice-tea-and-spices

**2025-06 (joint statement)**
- **Title:** 18 NGOs, including PAN Europe, PAN Germany, foodwatch, Corporate Europe Observatory, Friends of the Earth Europe and Greenpeace EU, ask the EU to ban production, export and import residues of banned pesticides, naming clothianidin as one of the most frequently detected.
- **Summary:** The statement says imidacloprid, thiamethoxam and clothianidin "were detected in nearly 500 samples of imported food analyzed by EU authorities in 2022". It credits the Commission's 2023 decision to lower import residue limits for clothianidin and thiamethoxam, which were "banned for environmental reasons, i.e unacceptable risks for bees". It calls for that approach to be extended to all banned pesticides.
- **Why it matters:** The effect is indirect. A broad EU NGO coalition explicitly endorses the view that clothianidin poses unacceptable environmental risk, which is a hazard-based position. Under "one substance, one assessment", that position is easy to carry into a biocide renewal debate. It also makes it harder for the EU to justify keeping clothianidin in another regime.
- **NGO and link:** PAN Europe (host) and co-signatories: https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/Letters/Banned%20pesticides_ENG_FINAL.pdf

**2024-12-02 (river data press release)**
- **Title:** UK conservation charities ask the government to stop neonicotinoid pollution of rivers and ban neonicotinoids in flea treatments, citing clothianidin at 29 of 45 tested river sites.
- **Summary:** Wildlife and Countryside Link worked with The Rivers Trust, The Wildlife Trusts, Greenpeace UK, Friends of the Earth, PAN UK and River Action. They analysed 2020–2024 river data and report clothianidin at 64% of sites. They describe it as "highly toxic to honeybees and persistent in water" and note it is also a breakdown product of thiamethoxam. Their demands include stronger enforcement, a legal end to emergency authorisations, and a ban on neonicotinoids in flea and tick treatments.
- **Why it matters:** The effect is indirect, but it is UK monitoring evidence of clothianidin persisting in surface water, and persistence and aquatic toxicity are hazard properties that carry over strongly between regimes. It also sets a precedent of NGOs targeting non-farm sources (flea treatments) for the same substances. Note that the river sources are mainly farm use and thiamethoxam breakdown, not biocides.
- **NGO and link:** Wildlife and Countryside Link and partners: https://www.wcl.org.uk/bee-killing-pesticides-polluting-majority-english-rivers.asp

### Tier 3: farm ban politics

**2024-12-21 (press release)**
- **Title:** The Wildlife Trusts welcome UK plans to legally prevent any future use of clothianidin, imidacloprid and thiamethoxam, and ask that emergency use never be authorised again, citing harm to bees.
- **Summary:** The Trusts welcome the UK Government's plan to legally prevent future use of the three neonicotinoids, explicitly naming clothianidin. They ask policymakers to "follow the science and never authorise the emergency use of neonicotinoids again".
- **Why it matters:** The effect is indirect and the item is UK-only. It is about plant protection products, and its concern is bees (hazard-based). It shows NGOs expect a permanent ban with no carve-outs. An EU biocide approval for the same substance is out of line with that expectation, although no NGO has yet pointed this out.
- **NGO and link:** The Wildlife Trusts (via Cheshire Wildlife Trust): https://www.cheshirewildlifetrust.org.uk/news/deadly-neonicotinoids-ban-must-bring-overdue-respite-bees-say-wildlife-trusts

## 5. What NGOs say is coming next

- **EU import residue limits:** NGOs describe the EU law cutting residue limits for clothianidin and thiamethoxam in imported food to trace levels as taking effect in 2026. foodwatch and the 2025-06 coalition want the same approach for all banned pesticides, plus an export ban. They expect the Commission to act on its February 2025 Vision for Agriculture and Food commitment.
- **Food and Feed Omnibus:** PAN Europe, foodwatch, CEO and HEAL are campaigning against unlimited approvals for pesticides and biocides while the proposal is negotiated in Parliament and Council (2026). No dates were given.
- **EU Bee Guidance:** PAN Europe and BeeLife want SCoPAFF to vote on the data requirements and uniform principles that would bring in the 2023 Bee Guidance, "in the next meeting". If adopted, wild-bee risk assessment would become standard. That could later be cited against neonicotinoids in other regimes.
- **UK legislation:** The Wildlife Trusts, Buglife and the Link coalition expect legislation to permanently prevent use of clothianidin, imidacloprid and thiamethoxam. Buglife and Link also want neonicotinoids out of flea treatments and other non-farm uses.
- **US (unverified, see section 3):** an EPA decision on NRDC's neonicotinoid tolerance petition is due by 2027-04-30.
