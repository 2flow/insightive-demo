# NGO activity report: lambda-cyhalothrin (CAS 91465-08-6), BPR PT18

## 1. Summary

5 of 7 top signals target the plant protection product (PPP) approval or PPP use, 1 targets water pollution, and 1 (national, Netherlands) targets household biocidal and garden products directly. No NGO has targeted the EU biocide approval itself. PAN Europe is the main actor. It keeps lambda-cyhalothrin on its "Toxic 12" immediate-ban list, and in March and April 2026 it opposed the Commission's latest proposal to extend the PPP approval. Its grounds are hazard-based: the substance is a PFAS, meets 2 PBT criteria (candidate for substitution) and is highly toxic to aquatic life. PAN Europe also uses the General Court rulings it won on repeated extensions (as PAN Europe describes them). The PFAS/TFA angle is also part of PAN Europe's 2025 Aarhus internal review request on approved PFAS pesticides. In the UK, Greenpeace (May 2026) made lambda-cyhalothrin the centre of a bee-toxicity campaign against Syngenta. In the Netherlands, PAN-NL (April 2025) put it on a 10-insecticide "black list" for home and garden products, including ant products for paths and terraces. Pressure is rising and hazard-led, and most of it is in the PPP regime. Its PFAS, PBT and aquatic-toxicity arguments carry over to the BPR file, where the substance is already a candidate for substitution.

## 2. Top signals

### Tier A: direct

**2025-04-07 — PAN Netherlands puts lambda-cyhalothrin on a "black list" of 10 home-and-garden insecticides and calls for a legal ban on synthetic pesticides for non-professional users. It cites PFAS status, neurotoxicity, bioaccumulation and possible endocrine disruption.**
`BPR` · `national:NL` · `direct` · `study/report`
- Summary: PAN-NL surveyed insecticides sold online (bol.com, AllesTegenOngedierte.nl) and in garden centres for use in house, garden and kitchen. It found lambda-cyhalothrin products sold against insects on vegetables and fruit, and against ants on paths, patios and terraces. Its black list combines biocide-type actives (permethrin, prallethrin, transfluthrin, tetramethrin) with lambda-cyhalothrin. PAN-NL asks retailers and webshops to phase the products out, and asks for legislation banning chemical pesticides for non-professional use. The report was broadcast on the AVROTROS consumer programme RADAR.
- Why it matters: this is the only signal aimed at consumer biocidal-type products (household insect and ant control) and not at farm use, so it acts directly on the PT18 product market. It is national, though, and asks for a ban on amateur use, not a change to the active substance approval. Its arguments are partly hazard-based (PFAS, PBT, candidate for substitution, neurotoxic), which carry over strongly, and partly use-specific (non-professional exposure), which carry over weakly. It could support restrictions on non-professional product authorisations in the Netherlands.
- NGO: PAN Netherlands, [press release (PDF)](https://www.pan-netherlands.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/persbericht.pdf); [research report (PDF)](https://www.pan-netherlands.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/onderzoeksrapport.pdf)

### Tier B: indirect, hazard-based

**2026-05-20 — Greenpeace UK blockades Syngenta's UK headquarters and calls lambda-cyhalothrin a bee-killing "forever chemical", calling for deep cuts in pesticide use and a ban on garden pesticides.**
`PPP` · `UK` · `indirect–hazard` · `campaign`
- Summary: on World Bee Day, Greenpeace activists blockaded Syngenta's site in Huddersfield. Greenpeace said one teaspoon of Syngenta's lambda-cyhalothrin product Hallmark is enough to kill 13 million bees, and that the substance was applied to more than 1 million hectares of British crops in 2024. The accompanying report "Our Poisoned Land" calls lambda-cyhalothrin highly toxic to bees, very toxic to aquatic life and a PFAS. It demands a 50% cut in pesticide use by 2030 and 80% by 2040, a ban on non-agricultural and private-garden pesticide use, and tighter controls on over-the-counter pet products. A follow-up release reported 41 arrests.
- Why it matters: this is the most visible public campaign naming the substance in the window, and its main argument is intrinsic hazard (bee and aquatic toxicity, PFAS persistence), which carries over strongly. Its scope is the UK and its demands are use reductions, not an EU approval decision, so its direct effect on the EU BPR renewal is limited. It adds to the public framing of lambda-cyhalothrin as a PFAS and highly hazardous pesticide.
- NGO: Greenpeace UK, [blockade news](https://www.greenpeace.org.uk/news/activists-blockade-pesticide-giant/); [41 arrested](https://www.greenpeace.org.uk/news/41-arrested-for-confronting-pesticide-giant-making-bee-killing-chemicals/); [Our Poisoned Land report (PDF)](https://www.greenpeace.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/OurPoisonedLand_text_v4.pdf)

**2026-04-30 — PAN Europe asks EU Member States to reject another extension of lambda-cyhalothrin's pesticide approval, citing its PFAS status, PBT properties and high aquatic toxicity, and court rulings against routine extensions. (TOP SIGNAL)**
`PPP` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`
- Summary: ahead of the 5–6 May 2026 meeting of the Standing Committee on Plants, Animals, Food and Feed (SCoPAFF), PAN Europe's Head of Science and Policy wrote to committee members about the draft regulation extending 19 PPP approvals (agenda item B.10). The letter says fluopicolide and lambda-cyhalothrin "are both PFAS substances and candidates for substitution (2 PBT criteria)" and that lambda-cyhalothrin is "highly toxic to the aquatic environment". It asks members to "oppose the extensions proposed for deltamethrin, chlorotoluron, tebuconazole, fluopicolide, and lambda-cyhalothrin". It cites, as PAN Europe describes them, General Court judgments (T-412/22, T-94/23, T-565/23) holding that extensions must be exceptional and temporary.
- Why it matters: this is the most direct EU-level push against the substance's continued approval in any regime, and it rests on intrinsic properties (PFAS, PBT, aquatic toxicity) that are the same in the BPR assessment. If the PPP approval ends or the legal attack on extensions succeeds, that would be a strong precedent for the BPR renewal, where the substance is also a candidate for substitution. Route: indirect, hazard-based.
- NGO: PAN Europe, [letter to SCoPAFF, 30 April 2026 (PDF)](https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/Letters/Letter_30042026_SCoPAFF%20letter%205%20May.pdf)

**2026-03-10 — PAN Europe criticises the Commission for proposing to extend lambda-cyhalothrin and four other hazardous pesticides despite a court ruling, and repeats that lambda-cyhalothrin should be banned immediately.**
`PPP` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`
- Summary: the blog post reports that the Commission proposal to SCoPAFF includes extensions for lambda-cyhalothrin, deltamethrin, fluopicolide, chlorotoluron and benzovindiflupyr. It notes that lambda-cyhalothrin and chlorotoluron are on PAN Europe's "Toxic 12" immediate-ban list. It presents the General Court judgment in PAN Europe's cases as requiring extensions to be exceptional and tied to real progress in the assessment, and links the issue to its opposition to the Food and Feed Safety Omnibus.
- Why it matters: this was the public start of the campaign that led to the April letter, and it shows lambda-cyhalothrin is a named priority, not just one name on a list. The argument is hazard-based, which carries over strongly. It also tests whether PAN Europe will challenge the extension in court.
- NGO: PAN Europe, [blog](https://www.pan-europe.info/blog/eu-continues-extending-permits-some-most-toxic-pesticides-despite-court-ruling-while-stalling)

**2025-11-20 — PAN Germany warns that pyrethroid insecticides, including lambda-cyhalothrin, are extremely toxic to aquatic life and calls for less use and stricter water-quality rules. It notes that pyrethroids also come from biocide and medicine uses.**
`water` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `statement`
- Summary: PAN Germany summarises scientific work on synthetic pyrethroids in European surface waters. It lists lambda-cyhalothrin among the agricultural pyrethroids and points out that many pyrethroids are also used in material protection and pest control (biocides) and in human and veterinary medicines. It reports scientists' recommendations to reduce pyrethroid use, improve analytical capacity and harmonise rules. It also reports that cypermethrin is a Water Framework Directive priority substance and that four more pyrethroids are proposed for listing.
- Why it matters: aquatic toxicity is a key end point in the PT18 environmental risk assessment. NGO pressure for pyrethroid environmental quality standards, and on detection limits, could tighten the risk envelope for biocidal emissions to sewage plants and surface water. The argument is hazard-based, so it carries over strongly, but the item is class-level and lambda-cyhalothrin is one of several named pyrethroids.
- NGO: PAN Germany, [article (DE)](https://pan-germany.org/pestizide/extrem-toxisch-fuer-wasserorganismen-pyrethroid-insektizide-im-fokus-von-wissenschaft-und-regulation/)

**2025-07-24 — PAN Europe files a formal Aarhus request asking the Commission to review and ban all approved PFAS pesticides, which its annex lists as including lambda-cyhalothrin, because they break down into the reprotoxic "forever chemical" TFA.**
`PPP` · `EU` · `indirect–hazard` · `formal request`
- Summary: PAN Europe filed a request for internal review under the Aarhus Regulation. It argues the Commission is breaking Articles 21 and 69 of Regulation 1107/2009 by not reviewing 28 approved PFAS active substances (CF3-containing; flufenacet and flutolanil excluded because they were already proposed for non-renewal). The 28 July 2025 annex to its TFA position paper lists lambda-cyhalothrin (CF3) with its hazard classifications (Acute Tox. 2 inhalation, Aquatic Acute and Chronic 1), its candidate-for-substitution status (bioaccumulative and toxic) and authorisation in 26 countries. The press release does not name lambda-cyhalothrin. Its inclusion in the 28 is inferred from the annex and the CF3 criterion.
- Why it matters: this attacks the PFAS/TFA property itself, which is the same whatever the regime. If it leads to Commission action under Article 21, TFA formation would become a likely issue in the BPR environmental assessment. Press reports say PAN Europe has taken the Commission's rejection to the General Court (case T-388/26, June 2026), but I could not confirm this on a PAN Europe page (see section 5).
- NGO: PAN Europe, [press release](https://www.pan-europe.info/press-releases/2025/07/pan-europe-demands-eu-commission-act-pfas-pesticides); [position paper annex listing lambda-cyhalothrin (PDF)](https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/briefings/Annex%20Position%20Paper%20TFA.pdf); [position paper (PDF)](https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/briefings/Position%20Paper%20TFA%20-%2028072025.pdf)

### Tier C: indirect, use-specific

**2025-04-08 — PAN Europe and PAN Netherlands expose highly toxic garden insecticides, including a lambda-cyhalothrin "Karate" vegetable spray, being sold to untrained consumers, and ask authorities to stop sales to the public.**
`PPP` · `national:NL` · `indirect–use` · `study/report`
- Summary: PAN Europe's blog presents PAN-NL's research (covering the Netherlands and Belgium) on garden products sold online and in garden shops. It names a lambda-cyhalothrin Karate garden product for vegetables and calls the substance "a very toxic PFAS pesticide". It argues that farmers need training and a spray licence while anyone can buy these products, and calls for a ban on selling toxic pesticides to the general public.
- Why it matters: the target is non-professional PPP use, so the argument is mainly about exposure from that use and carries over weakly to the BPR file. It does bring the PAN-NL home-and-garden campaign to the EU level.
- NGO: PAN Europe / PAN Netherlands, [blog](https://www.pan-europe.info/blog/highly-toxic-pesticides-sale-inexpert-users)

## 5. What NGOs say is coming next

- PPP extension: PAN Europe asked SCoPAFF on 5–6 May 2026 to oppose the extension. Its PFAS annex gives the PPP approval end as 31/08/2026. PAN Europe is likely to keep contesting each further extension, using the General Court case law on extensions it describes in its letters.
- PFAS pesticides litigation: PAN Europe said in July 2025 that its Aarhus request would let it go to the EU Court if the Commission failed to act. Press reports describe an action, PAN Europe v Commission (T-388/26), filed in June 2026 against the rejection of its review requests on 28 PFAS active substances. I could not confirm this on a PAN Europe page (unverified). If lambda-cyhalothrin is among the 28, as inferred above, the pending case is the most important legal risk to watch.
- TFA classification: PAN Europe's June 2026 SCoPAFF letter says ECHA's Risk Assessment Committee has confirmed TFA's classification as toxic for reproduction 1B and PMT/vPvM, and PAN Europe argues that all TFA-forming pesticides should be banned. Lambda-cyhalothrin is not named in that letter: [letter (PDF)](https://www.pan-europe.info/sites/pan-europe.info/files/public/resources/Letters/2026_06_29-30%20-%20SCoPAFF%20letter.pdf).
- Food and Feed Safety Omnibus: PAN Europe and foodwatch oppose the Omnibus provisions on MRLs and approvals (April 2026 letter). Relevant only as a horizontal policy.
- UK: Greenpeace UK is pushing for binding pesticide reduction targets of 50% by 2030 and 80% by 2040, and for a ban on garden pesticides.
- Netherlands: PAN-NL is calling for a legal ban on synthetic pesticides for non-professional users, including household insect and ant products, and for retailers to phase them out.
