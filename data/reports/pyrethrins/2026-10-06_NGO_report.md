# NGO activity on Pyrethrins / pyrethrum extract (BPR PT18/PT19)

## 1. Summary

There is 1 top signal. It targets consumer biocidal and garden products in the Netherlands (route `direct`, regime `cross-regime`). No NGO action targets the EU biocide approval or the EU plant-protection approval of pyrethrins. The only NGO naming pyrethrins in the window is PAN Netherlands. Its April 2025 "zwarte lijst" (black list) report says pyrethrin is too toxic for non-professional use. The report names pyrethrin/PBO fly and mosquito sprays and ant powder, and asks retailers to stop selling such products by 2027 and the Dutch government to ban chemical pesticides for amateur use. PAN-NL residue tests (bird seed, 2024; organic garden plants, April 2026) also found pyrethrins, but the concern in those reports is mainly the synergist PBO. Most NGO attention goes to synthetic pyrethroids (deltamethrin, cypermethrin, permethrin) and to the Omnibus deregulation of pesticide and biocide renewals. That work does not name pyrethrins. Pressure on the pyrethrins BPR file is low, and current NGO framing still often treats pyrethrum as the "natural" option.

## 2. Top signals

### Tier A: direct

**2025-04-07: PAN Netherlands wants pyrethrin taken off the shelves for home and garden use and asks retailers and the government to phase out consumer insecticide products, citing harm to bees and other insects, aquatic toxicity and possible endocrine effects**
`cross-regime` · `national:NL` · `direct` · `campaign`

- **Summary:** PAN-NL looked at the pesticides that private users can buy online and at Dutch garden centres (Welkoop, Praxis, Intratuin, bol.com and others). It published a "black list" of 10 insecticides it says are too harmful for non-professional use: deltamethrin, flupyradifurone, imidacloprid, lambda-cyhalothrin, permethrin, prallethrin, pyrethrin, spinosad, tetramethrin and transfluthrin. Four pyrethrin products are named. Two are plant-protection sprays (ECOstyle Promanal, Pokon Bio Polysect). Two are home pest-control products of the biocide type: Solabiol Natria fly and mosquito spray (pyrethrins 0.2% + PBO 1%) and ECOstyle ant powder based on "natuur-pyrethrum". The report says pyrethrin is not persistent but is "zeer toxisch voor aquatische en land insecten" (very toxic to aquatic and land insects), may be endocrine-disrupting, and may affect reproduction. It also flags PBO, which is often co-formulated. PAN-NL asks retailers and webshops to stop selling products with harmful substances to private users by 2027 and to adopt the black list. It asks the government to regulate toward a ban on chemical pesticides for non-professional use.
- **Why it matters for the BPR renewal:** This is the only NGO item in the window that names pyrethrins-based biocidal products directly. The route is direct, but the effect is national and falls on products. It targets retail availability and amateur-use authorisations in the Netherlands, not the EU active-substance approval, which runs to 2034/2035. The arguments mix hazard (insect and aquatic toxicity, suspected ED) and use (exposure from untrained private users). The hazard points carry over to other regimes, but they are cited from the PPDB rather than from new evidence, so their weight is limited. Short-term risk is reputational and retail delisting of consumer PT18 products in NL, plus possible pressure on Ctgb amateur-use authorisations. The "suspected ED" claim is worth watching if it is picked up elsewhere.
- **NGO and link:** PAN Netherlands, "PAN-NL zet 10 insecticiden op de zwarte lijst" (research report, 7 April 2025): https://www.pan-netherlands.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/onderzoeksrapport.pdf

## 5. What NGOs say is coming next

- PAN-NL asks Dutch retailers, garden centres and webshops to stop selling products with harmful insecticides (including pyrethrin) to private users by 2027, and asks the Dutch government to regulate toward a ban on chemical pesticides for non-professional use. Retail delisting announcements or a Dutch parliamentary/Ctgb follow-up would be the next things to watch.
- PAN-NL runs annual garden-plant residue tests (2021–2026) and calls for EU maximum residue limits for ornamental plants. Pyrethrins/PBO may appear again in the 2027 edition.
- Omnibus deregulation (Food and Feed Safety Omnibus, Omnibus X for biocides): PAN Europe and Générations Futures oppose unlimited approvals and the new biocontrol and renewal-exemption criteria. If natural or "biocontrol" substances such as pyrethrins end up on the exempt side, NGOs may start naming them. So far they name only synthetic pyrethroids.
- The pyrethroid health alerts (ANSES-based ED and neurodevelopment concerns) and the aquatic-toxicity campaign could spill over to pyrethrins if NGOs stop distinguishing natural from synthetic products. PAN-NL already groups them together for consumer use.
