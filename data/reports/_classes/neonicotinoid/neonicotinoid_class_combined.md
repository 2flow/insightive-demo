`cross-regime` · `international` · `indirect–hazard` · `study/report`

**2026-01-22 — Beyond Pesticides publicises a review finding male reproductive toxicity from neonicotinoids in rodents, calling the class a widespread hazard**
`cross-regime` · `international` · `indirect–hazard` · `study/report`
- Summary: A Beyond Pesticides blog post covers a George Mason University review of 21 rodent studies (2005–2025) on the male reproductive effects of neonicotinoids. All studies showed negative effects on male reproductive endpoints, such as reduced sperm count and motility and altered sperm morphology. Imidacloprid was the most studied (11 studies), followed by acetamiprid (4 studies, all with significant effects) and clothianidin (4 studies); thiacloprid, not a class member here, was studied in 2. The post cites the authors' view that the findings are relevant to human health and that neonicotinoids have endocrine-disrupting properties. Thiamethoxam and dinotefuran appear only in a list of widely used US neonicotinoids. The post closes by promoting organic practices.
- Why it matters: This builds a class-wide reproductive hazard narrative and gives specific support to the NGO case for classifying acetamiprid Repr. 1B. Under the BPR, Repr. 1B classification or ED identification would trigger exclusion or substitution at renewal. The argument rests on hazard grounds. The post is US-facing and summarises third-party research rather than asking for EU regulatory action.
