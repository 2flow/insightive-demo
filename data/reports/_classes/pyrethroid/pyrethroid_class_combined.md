**2026-04-04 — German consumer group says pyrethroid insecticide sprays should not be sold to consumers, citing nerve toxicity, suspected hormone effects and fatal poisoning of cats**
`BPR` · `national:DE` · `direct` · `statement`
- Summary: Verbraucherzentrale NRW updated its advice page (marked "Stand: 04. April 2026") after a reader's three cats died following use of a household insecticide spray. The page says most insecticide sprays contain pyrethroids, which can act as nerve toxins in humans and are suspected of affecting the hormone system. It says pyrethroid pest-control products should, as a precaution, not be supplied to consumers at all and should be used only by professional pest controllers. It also notes that Germany's self-service sales ban for insecticide sprays (in force since January 2025) does not cover repellent sprays.
- Why it matters: this targets consumer biocidal products (PT18) containing pyrethroids, the main market for several watch-list members. The demand rests on class hazard grounds (neurotoxicity, suspected endocrine disruption, toxicity to cats), not on one product. It supports pressure to restrict pyrethroid products to professional users at product-authorisation level in Germany. It is an updated advice page rather than a new campaign, and it builds on the joint VZ NRW / PAN Germany retail check of June 2025, which falls before the window.
- Named substances: none
- NGO: Verbraucherzentrale NRW, [Katze tot durch insektenspray worauf sollte man achten 43290](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.nrw/schadstoffe/schaedlinge/katze-tot-durch-insektenspray-worauf-sollte-man-achten-43290)

## 4. What NGOs say is coming next

- Cypermethrin Article 21 review (EU PPP) after the CJEU ruling: PAN Europe is pressing for withdrawal. The outcome will shape how hazard evidence on pyrethroids is weighed (this is the cypermethrin report's item; listed here as class context).
- PAN Netherlands' goal (from its April 2025 report, before the window) of no harmful insecticides offered for private use by 2027. Its black list names deltamethrin, lambda-cyhalothrin, permethrin, prallethrin, tetramethrin and transfluthrin. Watch for a 2026–2027 follow-up aimed at retailers or Ctgb.
- Germany: Verbraucherzentrale NRW and PAN Germany want pyrethroid sprays kept from consumers and have flagged the repellent loophole in the self-service sales ban. Watch for a renewed retail check or a demand to close the repellent loophole.
- EU water law (Directive (EU) 2026/805) adds bifenthrin, deltamethrin, esfenvalerate and permethrin as priority substances alongside cypermethrin. Transposition is due by 21 December 2027. Watch for NGO monitoring or compliance campaigns on pyrethroids in surface water.
- Food & Feed Safety Omnibus (EU PPP reform), which PAN Europe, Générations Futures and PAN Germany oppose. This is horizontal, not pyrethroid-specific, but it affects how long hazardous insecticides stay approved.
