`BPR` · `national:DE` · `direct` · `statement`
- Summary: Verbraucherzentrale NRW updated its advice page (marked "Stand: 04. April 2026") after a reader's three cats died following use of a household insecticide spray. The page says most insecticide sprays contain pyrethroids, which can act as nerve toxins in humans and are suspected of affecting the hormone system. It says pyrethroid pest-control products should, as a precaution, not be supplied to consumers at all and should be used only by professional pest controllers. The demand rests on class hazard grounds (neurotoxicity, suspected endocrine disruption, toxicity to cats), not on one product. It supports pressure to restrict pyrethroid products to professional users at product-authorisation level in Germany. It is an updated advice page rather than a new campaign, and it builds on the joint VZ NRW / PAN Germany retail check of June 2025, which falls before the window.

**2026-11-05 — Beyond Pesticides highlights study linking prenatal pyrethroid exposure to weaker newborn immune function, citing fewer and dying lymphocytes in cord blood**
`cross-regime` · `international` · `indirect–hazard` · `study/report`
- Summary: Beyond Pesticides wrote up a Journal of Global Health birth-cohort study (373 mother-newborn pairs). The study tested pyrethroid metabolites in maternal urine during pregnancy and found that higher exposure was linked to lower lymphocyte counts and more lymphocyte cell death in cord blood. The third trimester was the most sensitive period. The post treats pyrethroids as a class and makes no regulatory demand; it steers readers to organic food and to avoiding household and garden pesticide use. It adds a new hazard theme (developmental immunotoxicity) to the April 2026 cardiotoxicity post listed in the baseline.
- Why it matters: it adds a class-wide developmental immunotoxicity argument to the existing neurotoxicity, endocrine and cardiac themes, all of which EU NGOs can carry into BPR and PPP hazard debates. Household use, which is the PT18 market, is among the exposure sources the post names. The argument is hazard-based, but it comes from a US NGO with no EU demand, so its effect on the BPR approvals is indirect and weak.
- Named substances: none
- NGO: Beyond Pesticides, [daily news blog post](https://beyondpesticides.org/dailynewsblog/2026/11/prenatal-pyrethroid-insecticide-exposure-threatens-infant-immune-function-study-finds/)

`cross-regime` · `international` · `indirect–hazard` · `study/report`

It makes no explicit regulatory demand and steers readers towards organic food and non-chemical pest control.
- Why it matters: the post adds a class-wide human-health hazard theme (cardiotoxicity) to the existing neurotoxicity and endocrine arguments, and NGOs can carry it into EU hazard debates. It rests on hazard grounds and comes from a US NGO, so its effect on EU BPR renewals is indirect.
