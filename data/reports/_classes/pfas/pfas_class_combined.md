### Baseline 2026-10-21

10 top signals: 1 targets biocides directly (an NGO manifesto that calls for PFAS uses in biocides to be banned), 7 target other regimes on TFA/PFAS hazard grounds (CLP, food residues, water, national PPP litigation and formal requests), and 2 target the PPP regime on use or procedure grounds. The campaign is led by PAN Europe and its members (Générations Futures, GLOBAL 2000, PAN Germany), plus Deutsche Umwelthilfe in court. It centres on TFA as the breakdown product of PFAS pesticides. TFA's Repr. 1B classification by ECHA's risk committee (June 2026) and EFSA's much lower TFA intake value (July 2026) are now NGOs' main arguments for a grouped phase-out. Almost all of this work targets plant protection products. Biocides come up only in the PFAS manifesto, and only one watch-list member, lambda-cyhalothrin, is named in any top signal. The hazard argument (TFA is Repr. 1B and a groundwater metabolite) would apply directly to the BPR assessment of every member that carries a CF3 or OCF3 group.

`water` · `UK` · `indirect–hazard` · `study/report`
- Summary: Unearthed and Watershed obtained Environment Agency data through Freedom of Information requests. TFA was found in all 50 surface-water samples and in 43 of 49 groundwater samples. 42 groundwater samples were above 100 ng/L, the EU limit for relevant pesticide breakdown products, and the highest reading was 10,202 ng/L, near Finchingfield, Essex. Neither the press release nor the Unearthed article names any member; the full dataset is available only on request.
- Why it matters: this brings the baseline's EU TFA campaign to the UK, with official data showing widespread exceedance of the groundwater metabolite standard. It strengthens the argument that TFA-forming actives fail groundwater criteria, which would carry over to GB BPR and EU BPR assessments of members with CF3 or OCF3 groups. The grounds are hazard and exposure, not use. It makes no ban demand and does not mention biocides.
- Named substances: none
