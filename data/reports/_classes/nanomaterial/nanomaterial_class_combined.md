**2026-02-27 — Inf'OGM and AVICENN call for mandatory nano risk assessment and checks of pesticides, citing nanoparticles found in nearly all products tested**
`PPP` · `national:FR` · `indirect–hazard` · `study/report`
- Summary: Inf'OGM, co-publishing with Sciences Critiques, ran the investigation "Des nanoparticules en pagaille dans les pesticides" (English version "Nanoparticles galore in pesticides", 2026-03-02). It reports, as the NGO describes it, that an April 2025 ANSES/LNE exploratory study found sub-100 nm particles in all tested plant protection products and biocides "with the exception of one biocide". It stresses that the EU has no specific rules, no labelling duty and no particle-size testing requirement for nanopesticides. AVICENN (Mathilde Detcheverry) calls for "une évaluation robuste et systématique des risques associés aux nanos" before use, and for regular official checks. The named nanomaterials are copper, silver and titanium dioxide; silica is not mentioned.
- Why it matters: the argument rests on hazard grounds (nanoparticles crossing biological barriers, higher cytotoxicity of nano-copper, uncertain exposure) and on gaps in risk assessment. These arguments carry over to any nano active substance, including nano silica under the BPR. It also cites the ANSES finding that biocidal products contain nanoparticles. It is a French, PPP-focused item, however, and makes no BPR demand, so carry-over is moderate at most.
- Named substances: none
- NGO: Inf'OGM (with Sciences Critiques; demands from AVICENN). French, [Page on infogm.org](https://infogm.org/?p=15224)
- Also: [English: Page on infogm.org](https://infogm.org/?p=15244)
